Lindell withholds $825,000 recount payment, calls Minnesota plan 'unlawful'

MINNEAPOLIS (AP) — Mike Lindell's campaign on Monday withheld the $825,000 payment required to trigger a statewide hand recount of the Republican primary for Minnesota governor, calling the state's proposed recount scope "unlawful."

The MyPillow founder, who secured the endorsement of President Donald Trump for the race, finished second to Minnesota House Speaker Lisa Demuth in the August 11 primary, receiving 134,282 votes to Demuth's 179,683. On election night, Lindell said he would not concede until every vote was counted and that his team had spotted "anomalies" it needed time to review.

Lindell's team argues that the Secretary of State's plan would count only the votes cast for Lindell and Demuth, leaving out the votes for the other five Republican candidates on the ballot. The campaign cited Minnesota Statute 204C.35, which it says defines a recount as determining "the number of votes validly cast for the office or question to be recounted."

Lindell has asked the state to waive the payment deadline until a court can rule on the scope of the recount.

"Our office provided the contingent recount plan to Mr. Lindell after it was approved by the State Canvassing Board on Aug. 18," a spokesperson for Minnesota Secretary of State Steve Simon said. "The plan follows state law and administrative rules on recount procedures, and is consistent with plans of its kind for the past two decades."

The contingency plan, released last week, names Paul Linnell, the office's director of elections, as state recount official, with authority to appoint deputy officials at counting sites across the state. The recount would be open to the public and media.

There have been no official findings of fraud or irregularity in the primary results. Lindell, who described his recount effort as a "gateway to getting rid of" electronic voting machines, had gathered with supporters at the Hilton Hotel in downtown Minneapolis on election day.

Demuth will face Sen. Amy Klobuchar in the November 3 general election. Governor Tim Walz, who is not running again, presides over a state where the recount dispute has become its own campaign story.
