At least 7 dead after days of historic flooding in Indiana

INDIANAPOLIS (AP) — Days of storms and record-setting flooding in Indiana left at least seven people dead as floodwaters receded Sunday and the state began recovery.

A 4-year-old boy, three men and three women were among those who had died since the storms began Aug. 11, said Liz Woods, a spokesperson for the state Department of Homeland Security.

Among the latest confirmed victims was James Briar, a 31-year-old cyclist found dead early Sunday in a hole that opened up on a washed-out road in Henry County, east of Indianapolis. Henry County Coroner Brian Clark said Briar appears to have gone around a barricade and fallen into the collapsed road in New Castle. His body was discovered just before 4 a.m. Aug. 16 and he was pronounced dead at the scene.

Other victims included Matthew Morey, 19, of Delaware County, who drowned in the Mississinewa River, and a 58-year-old woman in Delaware County whose home was struck when a tree came through her bedroom window during the storm.

President Donald Trump approved an emergency declaration for Indiana on Saturday, which allows the Federal Emergency Management Agency to provide resources to the state after severe storms, straight-line winds, tornadoes and flooding that swept over half of the state's 92 counties.

Heavy rain pushed the White River to a crest of approximately 24.60 feet in Hamilton County around 3:30 a.m. Aug. 15, breaking a record set more than 100 years ago, county officials said.

At the peak of the flooding, more than 350,000 residents lost power, including almost all of Lake County, and the Indiana National Guard was deployed to assist local responders.
