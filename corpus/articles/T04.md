Hawk Fire spreads near Reno, 42,000 ordered to evacuate

RENO, Nevada (AP) — A fast-growing wildfire that began Saturday in the hills northwest of Reno had burned more than 15,000 acres by late Sunday with zero containment, and Nevada Governor Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency in Washoe County.

The Hawk Fire was first reported around 11:25 a.m. Saturday near Hawk Meadow Trail in the Peavine Peak area of the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest, where rugged foothills ringed by homes and businesses sit near the California state line. Officials attributed the fire to human causes.

Strong winds made it difficult for firefighters to hold the line as the blaze spread across the foothills. By Sunday the "GO NOW" evacuation zone had expanded to the edge of the University of Nevada campus, with roughly 42,000 residents under evacuation orders and an additional 45,000 people in the adjacent warning zone. Authorities urged people to be prepared to stay away for several days.

More than 13,700 residences sit inside the evacuation area. Six people were injured.

"I have declared a state of emergency in Washoe County as we respond to the Hawk Fire, which is spreading quickly and threatening homes and structures," Governor Joe Lombardo said. "Please heed all warnings from local officials, evacuate immediately if you are ordered to do so, and do not put yourself or first responders at risk."

The declaration directs all state agencies to work through the Nevada Emergency Operations Center, and the governor authorized one CH-47 Chinook helicopter, one UH-60 Black Hawk and 72 National Guard security personnel to support the response.

US Highway 395 was closed between Parr Boulevard and Red Rock Road as the fire line shifted.
