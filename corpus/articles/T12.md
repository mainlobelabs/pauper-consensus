US launches 'Operation Economic Outcast' against Iran

WASHINGTON (AP) — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Monday announced a new campaign of financial sanctions against Iran, warning every country that does business with the Islamic Republic to sever those ties or face retaliation from the United States.

Bessent dubbed the campaign "Operation Economic Outcast" and an "economic D-Day," likening it to the Allied landings in France in the Second World War. "Our objective is to sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone," he said.

The announcement followed President Donald Trump's pledge last week to unleash an "economic D-Day" against Tehran and his declaration that countries doing business with Iran would face "tremendous economic consequences."

The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels in multiple jurisdictions, including in the United Arab Emirates, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland, accusing them of enabling the Iranian regime's oil, nuclear, missile and cyber networks. Treasury said the new sanctions target five sectors: cryptocurrency used by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and regime insiders, weapons-related technologies, gold, Iran's airlines and shipping.

Bessent said the United States had been engaged in "quiet diplomacy" with Iran's trade partners, warning that any entity that facilitates money laundering on behalf of Iran will be removed from the US dollar system. "No one is above the reach of US sanctions," he said.

China, Turkey and the United Arab Emirates are Iran's largest trade partners. Ahead of Monday's announcement, the United Arab Emirates said it was ending all trade relations with Iran.

The rial fell to a record low against the dollar this week, trading at roughly 2.02 million rials per dollar. The US-Israel war on Iran has now entered its sixth month, with the military front at a stalemate.
