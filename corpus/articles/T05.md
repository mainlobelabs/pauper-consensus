US national debt tops $40 trillion for the first time

WASHINGTON (Reuters) — Total US debt has topped $40 trillion for the first time, the Treasury Department said on Wednesday, drawing fresh warnings that a fiscal crisis is brewing as ballooning costs for social safety-net programs and interest payments far outstrip revenues held back by tax cuts.

The Treasury's latest daily cash and debt balances statement showed total public debt outstanding at $40.047 trillion on Tuesday, a total that includes Treasury securities held by the public of $32.266 trillion and intra-governmental debt holdings of $7.782 trillion.

The milestone has arrived faster than forecasters expected. A decade ago, the national debt stood at about $19.4 trillion, so the government's total borrowing has more than doubled in ten years.

The Congressional Joint Economic Committee calculated that the debt is now growing at roughly $90,000 every second. At a population of about 343 million, the $40 trillion total works out to approximately $117,000 per person.

Treasury also reported a $432.3 billion deficit for July, the highest monthly total in more than five years, with the year-to-date shortfall nearing $1.8 trillion.

The Washington Post reported that the landmark figure was reached months earlier than some models predicted, in part because of billions of dollars in lost revenue from President Donald Trump's invalidated tariffs.

The public share of the debt is now near 100 percent of GDP, and the total passed $30 trillion just four and a half years ago.
