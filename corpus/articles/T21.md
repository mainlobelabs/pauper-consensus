US 50% tariffs on Canadian goods take effect; Carney vows retaliation

OTTAWA (Reuters) — Canada will impose tariffs on US goods in retaliation for the 50 percent levies that took effect just after midnight on Saturday, Prime Minister Mark Carney said, after three days of trade talks between the two neighbours collapsed.

The "dollar for dollar" tariffs on imports of US steel, dairy, household appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper and electronics will take effect on September 8, the Tuesday after Labor Day.

The new US tariffs cover roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports, about 5 percent of Canada's annual trade with the United States, and span sectors from wine to hockey equipment. They were ordered under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 and stack on top of a 10 percent levy imposed earlier this month.

President Donald Trump announced the 50 percent tariffs in an executive order on July 20, accusing Canada of "discriminatory treatment" in its trade policies. They were initially set to take effect on August 19 but Trump granted a three-day pause, saying the two countries were close to a deal.

The pause ran out without an agreement. Late on Friday, Carney said he was suspending trade negotiations and ordering Canadian negotiators back to Ottawa.

"We were not prepared to compromise Canada's sovereignty or undermine our key industries," Carney said in a 22-minute address to Canadians. "You're at war when you're attacked, and we got attacked."

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said there are no future talks planned with Canada and that Washington is moving forward with measures to respond to Canadian retaliation. The breakdown complicates the future of the USMCA free trade deal.

Ontario Premier Doug Ford, whose province is hit hardest by the tariffs, said he backed Carney's position "tariff for tariff, dollar for dollar."
