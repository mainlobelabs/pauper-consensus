Somali Airlines to resume flights after 35-year hiatus

MOGADISHU — Somalia's national carrier, Somali Airlines, is expected to resume flights in September 2026, more than three decades after it stopped operating following the collapse of the country's central government in 1991.

Ahmed Moalin, director of the Somali Civil Aviation Authority, said Saturday that preparations to relaunch the carrier had been completed and that flights were expected to begin next month.

"Somali Airlines is in the final stage," Moalin said. "I think the first flight will begin in September."

Somali Airlines, established in 1964 and once known as the "White Star," was the country's flag carrier, operating domestic and international routes, before its operations were halted by the civil war that followed the ouster of President Siad Barre in 1991.

The planned revival follows several earlier missed restart targets. The government announced an agreement involving two Airbus A320 aircraft in July 2025 as the first step toward reviving the carrier, and in October 2025 the transport ministry said the airline would begin services by the end of that year. That did not happen.

Mogadishu's Aden Adde International Airport is currently served by international carriers including Ethiopian Airlines, Qatar Airways and Turkish Airlines, and for more than 30 years air travel in Somalia has largely been handled by private and foreign airlines.

Officials say the return of the national carrier is meant to improve domestic and regional connectivity, strengthen links with the Somali diaspora, create jobs and support trade.
