Tupac Shakur murder trial: prosecution builds case on Davis's own words

LAS VEGAS (AP) — The trial of Duane "Keffe D" Davis, the only person ever charged in the 1996 murder of rapper Tupac Shakur, began Monday at the Clark County District Court Regional Justice Center in Las Vegas, 30 years after one of America's most high-profile unsolved killings.

Davis, 63, the former leader of the South Side Compton Crips, has pleaded not guilty to one count of murder with a deadly weapon and could face life in prison if convicted.

"Let's be clear, Duane Davis did not pull the trigger. But he did plan the shooting in retaliation for the beating of his nephew," Chief Deputy District Attorney Binu Palal told jurors in his opening statement. "Remarkably, you will learn that from Duane Davis himself."

Prosecutors allege Davis was the "shot-caller" who conceived the plan to exact revenge on Shakur and on Death Row Records CEO Suge Knight after Shakur and his entourage beat Davis's nephew, Orlando Anderson, outside the MGM Grand hours before the September 7, 1996, drive-by. Shakur was shot multiple times in a black BMW that Knight was driving; Knight was grazed in the head. Shakur died six days later.

The case rests heavily on Davis's own statements over two decades: a confidential 1998 interview with the FBI, a 2008 interview with Los Angeles police detectives who were investigating the killing of rapper Biggie Smalls, a 2009 interview with Las Vegas police, and a 2019 memoir he co-authored, Compton Street Legend. In the 2008 interview, Davis said he passed a gun to the back seat of the car, and that his nephew Anderson fired.

Defense attorney Michael Sanft has attacked his own client's credibility, calling the years of statements and the memoir fiction. "They are telling you, they are representing as fact, when it's really fiction," Sanft told jurors.

On the second day of the trial, jurors were shown photos of Shakur's body and the casings recovered at the scene, and a forensic pathologist testified that a bullet entered Shakur's chest near his armpit and passed through his lung.

James "Mob James" McDonald, a former member of Shakur's entourage, took the stand. Knight, who is on the prosecution's witness list, has said he wants no part in the trial.

The prosecution is expected to call between 35 and 45 witnesses, and the trial is expected to last up to six weeks.
