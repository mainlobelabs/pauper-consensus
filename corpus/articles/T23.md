Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan sentenced to life in prison

HONG KONG/SHENZHEN (Reuters) — The founder of China Evergrande Group, the world's most-indebted property developer, was sentenced to life in prison by a Chinese court on Thursday, five years after the firm's collapse shook the nation's economy and financial markets.

The Shenzhen Intermediate People's Court said it convicted 67-year-old Hui Ka Yan, also known as Xu Jiayin, of large-scale financial fraud in inflating the group's assets and concealing its liabilities, along with illegal fundraising and bribery. Hui, once Forbes Asia's richest man, pleaded guilty in April to eight charges.

The court ordered the confiscation of all of Hui's personal property, with political rights revoked for life, and said restitution for victims takes priority over state fines and asset seizures.

Evergrande Group was fined 8.82 billion yuan ($1.31 billion) and Evergrande Real Estate Group, its major mainland subsidiary, was fined 7 billion yuan ($1.04 billion), a combined 15.82 billion yuan (about $2.35 billion).

Five other senior executives were sentenced to prison terms ranging from six to 18 years, and 56 people in total were sentenced in connection with the collapse, state media reported.

Hui was separately fined 47 million yuan and banned from China's securities markets for life by the China Securities Regulatory Commission in 2024 over inflating company financial results.

At its peak, Evergrande employed about 200,000 people, and its default in 2021 triggered a property crisis that spread across the industry.
