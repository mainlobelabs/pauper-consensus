US removes Syria from state sponsors of terrorism list

DAMASCUS (Reuters) — The United States on Monday removed Syria from its list of state sponsors of terrorism, lifting what Damascus says was the last major barrier to private-sector investment in the country as Washington deepens ties to the new Syrian government.

The removal, posted on the U.S. Treasury's website, took effect after a 45-day congressional review period triggered when President Donald Trump formally notified Congress on July 8 of his intention to rescind the decades-old designation. Syria had been on the list since 1979, when Washington accused the government of former President Hafez al-Assad of supporting armed groups, and it remained throughout the rule of his son, Bashar al-Assad, who was toppled by rebels led by current President Ahmed al-Sharaa in December 2024.

" These actions were all taken in recognition of the positive actions taken and further commitments by the Syrian government under President Ahmed al-Sharaa to fully distance Syria from acts of international terrorism," U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in a statement.

Monday's announcement also revoked the designation of al-Nusrah Front, the group formerly led by al-Sharaa and known as Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), as a global terrorist organization. HTS was once al-Qaeda's official branch in Syria. The move leaves Cuba, North Korea and Iran as the three countries designated by Washington as state sponsors of terrorism.

Trump notified Congress after meeting al-Sharaa in Ankara in July, telling the Syrian president in a letter that he had promised to remove all barriers preventing Syria from rebuilding.

Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shibani said there is "no longer any obstacle to investment, doing business and rebuilding economic life in Syria," and the country's central bank chief, Safwat Raslan, said the government is building a modern financial system.

US envoy Tom Barrack called the end of the designation a decisive step in Syria's journey "from isolation to partnership."
