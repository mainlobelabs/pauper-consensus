Lake Kariba ferry disaster: death toll rises to 80

HARARE (AP) — The death toll from an overloaded ferry that capsized on Lake Kariba in Zimbabwe last week rose to 80 after eight more bodies were recovered on Sunday, police said.

The government-run passenger ferry, the Mbuya Nehanda, overturned in choppy water on Tuesday while travelling from the town of Kariba toward rural and fishing communities in the country's northwest, where roads are damaged and public transport is scarce. The disaster is believed to be the deadliest of its kind in the country's history.

Officials say the boat was authorised to carry 90 people, but reports indicate it was carrying 114 adults and five crew members, together with an unspecified number of children. Zimbabwe's disaster management agency said 77 people were rescued.

Search teams recovered 25 bodies on Saturday, taking the toll to 72, before Sunday's further recoveries. Police said the search is continuing, guided by information from relatives, and that the exact passenger manifest has not been established.

Among the dead are children, according to authorities, and hundreds of people gathered at the district hospital in Kariba to identify the bodies of their relatives. The town of Kariba sits nearly 280 kilometres northwest of the capital, Harare, and the ferry was a vital link for the rural communities living around the lake, which shares its waters with Zambia.

Survivors said they had pleaded with the driver to turn back as the water turned rough.
