France's top court blocks social media ban for under-15s

PARIS (Reuters) — France's top court on Friday blocked a bill banning social media access for under-15s, saying it infringed upon freedom of expression and delivering a setback for President Emmanuel Macron, who asked his government to rewrite the legislation.

The Constitutional Council, ruling on two parliamentary referrals, found that Article 1 of the law "constitutes an infringement that is neither appropriate, necessary, nor proportionate" to the freedom of expression and communication of children under 15.

The law would have barred children younger than 15 from opening a social media account from September 1, and platforms would have had to close existing accounts within four months using age verification approved by the French privacy regulator. French lawmakers approved the ban in July, which would have made France the first European country to enact such a measure, after Australia barred under-16s from platforms including Facebook, TikTok and YouTube in December.

The council said that by prohibiting minors under 15 from accessing certain online services, the law inherently requires every person, even an adult, to prove their age before accessing them, and the legislature did not establish the conditions and limits of that proof. It also said the ban's scope was too broad, reaching collaboration tools, communication apps and social games whose risks for minors are not established.

The Elysee said Macron ordered Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu to re-work the bill to take the Constitutional Council's concerns into account. Macron is determined the reform take effect before Spring 2027, when France holds a presidential election in which he cannot run for a third term.
