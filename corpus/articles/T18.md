Chinese robot beats Usain Bolt's 100m world record at Beijing games

BEIJING (Reuters) — A Chinese humanoid robot ran 100 metres in 9.39 seconds in a preliminary heat at the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing on Saturday, faster than the men's world record held by Jamaican sprinting great Usain Bolt.

Tiangong Ultra, developed by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center, won its heat at the opening of the second edition of the games, held at Beijing's National Speed Skating Oval, known as the Ice Ribbon. The robot overtook Honor's Lightning near the finish after falling behind early in the race; Lightning finished in 9.47 seconds, also faster than the 9.58-second record Bolt set at the 2009 World Athletics Championships.

At the end of the dash, the robots slammed into a cushion wall and fell to the ground to stop, and footage showed them being carried off on stretchers.

Before the opening, a humanoid robot from Chinese smartphone maker Honor completed a 100-metre sprint in 9.32 seconds during a trial of the games at a peak speed of 14.5 metres per second.

At last year's first edition of the games, the best time in the event was 21.50 seconds, so the new mark marks a leap in the running speed of humanoid robots.

More than 2,000 robots from 666 teams are competing in Beijing across 51 events. The games are being held the same week as the 2026 World Robot Conference, where thousands of products are on display.
