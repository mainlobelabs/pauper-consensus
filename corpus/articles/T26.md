Gang attack near Port-au-Prince leaves 47 dead, 50 kidnapped

PORT-AU-PRINCE (AP) — At least 47 people were killed and more than 50 others kidnapped after armed men attacked a once-peaceful community near Haiti's capital earlier this week, the United Nations said Tuesday.

A powerful gang coalition raided the hillside community of Kenscoff, in the hills above Port-au-Prince, late on Sunday, setting fire to homes and killing residents, according to Kenscoff Mayor Jean Massillon, who called it a night of terror.

U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres condemned the attack, noting that 22 of the victims were reportedly executed in a church compound where displaced people had sought shelter. At least five of the 47 people killed were children, the U.N. Integrated Office in Haiti said.

It is believed to be one of the largest mass kidnappings in Haiti in recent years, and one gang leader is threatening to kill the hostages if authorities kill any gang members as they try to secure the community.

Gang members targeted a church and homes, including those owned by Dr. Jean "Bill" Pape, a renowned doctor who runs a non-governmental organization in Haiti that helps patients with HIV and tuberculosis. The mayor said two government employees were among the killed, including his cousin, who served as his security detail.

The mayor said members of Viv Ansanm, the gang coalition that the United States has designated as a foreign terrorist organization, also set fire to 25 homes.

Gang violence has displaced a record 1.5 million people across Haiti, where more than 3,050 people were reported killed from January to June, the U.N. said.
