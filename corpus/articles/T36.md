Panama Canal to limit ship transits due to El Niño drought

PANAMA CITY (DW) — The Panama Canal will cut the number of ships passing through it starting next month because of drought caused by the El Niño phenomenon, the operator of the strategic waterway linking the Atlantic and Pacific said on Thursday.

The Panama Canal Authority (ACP) announced temporary modifications to the locks' transit capacity, including adjustments to its daily auctions, walking back a previous pledge not to limit vessel passage. Under the new measures, the canal will limit daily transits to 34 vessels, down from the current cap of 36, as of September 3. From September 15, the number of ships permitted will drop to 32.

The cuts are driven by low water levels in the two artificial lakes, Gatun and Alajuela, that feed the canal. Rainfall between May and August was about 34% below the historical average for the period, the canal operator said, and water contributions to the basin were 44% below the historical average.

Central America is in the throes of a severe drought driven by El Niño, a weather pattern that warms surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean. Scientists expect the 2026-2027 El Niño to be particularly strong, possibly among the strongest on record, and Panama Canal administrator Ricaurte Vasquez warned it could last much longer than usual.

The 50-mile (80-kilometer) canal carries around 5% of global maritime trade. Through June 2026 it averaged 35 daily transits, against a capacity of roughly 40 per day.

The waterway was also hit by severe drought in 2023-2024. In 2023, lake water levels were so low that the operator cut daily ship transits from 38 to 22.

The ACP also postponed further draft restrictions: the maximum authorized draft of 14.63 meters in the Neopanamax locks was delayed to September 2, and the reduction to 14.48 meters to October 1.
