Ebola outbreak in DR Congo becomes deadliest in its history

KINSHASA (AFP) — The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has killed 2,325 people, surpassing the death toll of the 2018-2020 outbreak to become the deadliest in the country's history, public health officials said on Sunday.

DR Congo's Institute of Public Health said it had recorded 4,945 confirmed cases, including 101 in the previous 24 hours. The latest government data shows that the total number of deaths has surpassed the 2,299 deaths recorded in the 2018-2020 outbreak, which was previously the country's worst on record.

The outbreak, declared by national authorities on 15 May, is the 17th to have emerged in DR Congo since Ebola was discovered 50 years ago. It became the country's largest in terms of total confirmed cases in late July, and researchers say it may have been spreading undetected since February.

It is the fastest-growing outbreak in the known history of the virus: the world's deadliest, in West Africa in 2014-2016, took close to five months to reach 1,000 fatalities, while the current outbreak reached 2,000 deaths in under three months. The World Health Organization has said the outbreak is on track to surpass the 2014-2016 outbreak, which killed 11,325 people across Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone.

The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain. As of 19 August, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control reported 5,290 confirmed cases and 2,516 deaths, with 837 people hospitalised in Ituri, North Kivu and Haut-Uele provinces. The virus has spread to Uganda, and Europe has recorded its first imported case.

Infections among health workers continue, with at least 155 confirmed cases, including 45 deaths.

On Thursday, the UN's International Coordinating Group, led by the WHO, allocated 70,000 doses of the Ervebo vaccine, developed by Merck, to DR Congo. Ervebo is approved for the Zaire strain, used in the 2018 and 2020 DRC outbreaks, and has not been evaluated against Bundibugyo, the strain in this outbreak, so the doses will be used in a Phase 3 trial of the Zaire vaccine against Bundibugyo. The ICG had already allocated 56,000 Ervebo doses for DRC outbreaks between 2021 and July 2026.
