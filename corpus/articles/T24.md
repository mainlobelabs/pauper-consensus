Kenya helicopter crash kills seven, including Ecuador's intelligence chief

NAIROBI (AFP) — All seven people on board a safari helicopter, six passengers and a pilot, were killed on Wednesday when the aircraft crashed in the foothills of Mount Ololokwe in Samburu County, central Kenya, authorities said.

Kenya's Civil Aviation Authority said the group's Eurocopter EC130 B4, operated by Lady Lori Helicopters, crashed at 9:13 a.m. (06:13 GMT) after departing the Loisaba Conservancy on a scenic charter toward the Ewaso Ng'iro area. The Kenya Red Cross said difficult terrain and a fire at the crash site hampered search and recovery efforts.

Among the dead were Michele Sensi-Contugi, 44, director general of Ecuador's strategic intelligence center, and his wife, Stephany Hollihan, 42, a fashion designer who also held US citizenship.

A US State Department spokesperson said five of the six passengers killed were US citizens, and NBC News reported that one of them was Jose Suarez, a prominent television executive. The other American victims were from South Florida.

Sensi-Contugi, a former businessman and close associate of Ecuador's right-wing President Daniel Noboa, was appointed head of national intelligence in January 2024. Noboa declared three days of national mourning and ordered a state funeral.

The pilot was identified as Josh Outram, an American.

Mount Ololokwe is a sacred mountain for the Samburu people, its summit rising 2,000 metres above sea level, and it is a popular hiking destination where companies offer helicopter flyovers.
