Brazil orders Discord to suspend livestreaming after teen's death

RIO DE JANEIRO (AFP/Reuters) — Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) on Wednesday ordered Discord to suspend its "Go Live" livestreaming feature across the country after a 13-year-old girl was encouraged to take her own life during a broadcast on the platform.

The order gives Discord three business days to comply, and the platform can appeal within 10 business days. Discord itself will not be blocked; text messaging and voice channels continue. What stops is the livestreaming feature and similar video-sharing tools, which must remain suspended until the company proves it has put adequate protective measures in place for minors, which may include age verification.

The investigation began on Friday after the death of the 13-year-old girl in central-western Brazil. Authorities say that during a 45-minute Discord livestream she was pressured to end her life by others in the server she was in.

The ANPD said it found "robust evidence" that Discord failed to take reasonable steps to prevent minors from being exposed to content promoting violence, self-harm and suicide. It said the company's livestreaming system prevents it from accessing the content of video streams while they are happening, which means it cannot automatically detect violations in real time and instead relies on automated systems the agency described as flawed, along with reports from users.

The regulator also pointed to a product change in early March, when Discord introduced end-to-end encryption to its communications, making the company's access to in-stream content even more limited.

Discord faces fines of up to 50 million reais (about $9.67 million) per violation.

The agency said livestreams have repeatedly been used in connection with serious crimes in Brazil, and that the suspension is a preventive measure while the company strengthens safeguards.
