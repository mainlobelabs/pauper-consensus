Central African Republic closes gold mine that killed more than 100

BANGUI (AP) — The Central African Republic's government on Saturday shut down a gold mining site that killed more than 100 people when it collapsed earlier this week, citing non-compliance with regulatory procedures.

Ruffin Benam Beltoungou, the minister of mines and geology, announced the closure of the Zamboye site in the western part of the country, citing "non-compliance with mining standards and non-compliance with administrative directives," as an investigation continues.

The artisanal mine caved in Tuesday in the village of Zamboye, near the border with Cameroon. Local officials and the Red Cross said Wednesday that more than 100 people had died in the collapse and warned the toll was likely to rise, while the mines ministry put the official toll at 49, a breakdown of 34 Central Africans, 13 Cameroonians and two Chadians.

The minister visited the site on Wednesday. Seven injured people were taken to a hospital in Baboua, about 50 kilometres from the mining site, and rescue operations were at times suspended because the mine continued to collapse and heavy rains had flooded lower sections of the pit.

The site, locally known as Moscow-RCA, is a sprawling open-pit mine beside the Kadei River, which forms part of the border with Cameroon, and thousands of people in the area depend on it. The Zamboye site had been closed before the accident, but illegal miners still worked there.

The Baboua prosecutor's office opened a judicial investigation to establish the cause of the collapse and who was running the site. Search for survivors by local workers was hampered by a lack of expertise and equipment for the scale of the disaster.
