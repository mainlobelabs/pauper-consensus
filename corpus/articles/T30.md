Venezuela says 131 political prisoners freed amid post-Maduro talks

CARACAS (AFP) — Venezuela says it has freed 131 people considered political prisoners, in a sign of progress for national reconciliation following the United States' removal of former President Nicolas Maduro.

Those released had been granted "alternative measures from incarceration" under the Program for Peace and Democratic Coexistence, the country's interim government said on Friday, in a statement distributed by communications minister Miguel Perez Pirela. The releases took place at El Rodeo I prison in Guatire, in the Miranda state.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the release of more than 130 political prisoners was "a crucial step for the nation's reconciliation process." Since the US overthrew and detained Maduro on January 3, 1,046 Venezuelans have been released, Rubio added.

The move comes after the first round of US-brokered reconciliation talks between the government and the opposition, which kicked off last week and wrapped up on Wednesday. The talks are being led by opposition figure Dinorah Figuera and National Assembly President Jorge Rodriguez, the brother of interim President Delcy Rodriguez, who took over as Maduro's deputy.

The opposition movement Vente Venezuela said it had independently verified the release of 43 detainees as of 4:58 p.m. local time and that it was continuing to review individual cases. The Foro Penal rights group confirmed the release of at least 40 political prisoners.

As of Monday, Foro Penal said 391 political prisoners remained behind bars.

Opposition figure Juan Pablo Guanipa welcomed the releases but demanded that all political prisoners be freed "without exception, without delay, and promptly," saying each person returning home is "an immense relief" for families who have "suffered too much."
