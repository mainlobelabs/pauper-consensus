Renaissance paintings by Antonello da Messina stolen from Sicilian museum

MESSINA (AP) — Four of the most important works of art by the 15th-century Renaissance master Antonello da Messina were stolen during a national holiday from a museum in the Sicilian port city of Messina.

Thieves gained access to the Regional Interdisciplinary Museum, or MuMe, at around 9:50 p.m. on Saturday as the city celebrated Ferragosto and the La Vara religious festival. Three or four masked intruders entered through a side gate where part of the perimeter railing was bent or cut, forced an exterior door, and reached the rooms where the works were kept. The museum's alarms and video surveillance systems were functioning, and the robbery took just a few minutes.

All five surviving wooden panels of the San Gregorio Polyptych, an altarpiece signed and dated to 1473, were removed from their frames, but the thieves abandoned two of them against a low wall outside the museum, where a citizen noticed them and called police. The third stolen work was a small double-sided panel taken from an armored display case, depicting the Virgin and Child with a Franciscan monk on one side and the dead Christ on the other.

The San Gregorio Polyptych was made for the Monastero di San Gregorio, destroyed in the 1908 earthquake, and it was the only work by the painter that had never left his home city of Messina. Art expert Alberto Fiz valued the stolen works at 70 to 80 million euros, up to about $93 million.

"We are devastated by what happened. They were among Antonello da Messina's most important and best-known works. It is a tremendous loss for the museum, the city, the community and the art world," MuMe director Marisa Mercurio told ANSA.

The Messina prosecutor's office has opened an investigation into aggravated theft of artworks, and investigators are focusing on the possibility of an internal accomplice, after the alarm was apparently deactivated by a custodian. The heist is believed to have been carried out on commission for an art dealer.

Sicilian Regional President Renato Schifani described the theft as an "exceptional criminal act" and an "intolerable affront to universal cultural heritage," and launched an internal inspection. Days earlier, Italian authorities had recovered three stolen Cezanne, Renoir and Matisse paintings.
