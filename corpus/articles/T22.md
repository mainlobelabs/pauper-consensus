Bangladesh elects its president in first contested vote in 35 years

DHAKA (AP) — Bangladesh's parliament on Thursday elected ruling party veteran Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir as the country's 23rd president in the first contested presidential election in 35 years.

Alamgir, 78, a stalwart of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), won with 255 votes, defeating Colonel (retd) Oli Ahmed, 84, the chairman of the Liberal Democratic Party and the nominee of the 11-party opposition alliance led by Jamaat-e-Islami, who received 88 votes. Of the 349 members of parliament eligible to vote, 343 cast ballots, and the chief election commissioner, AMM Nasir Uddin, declared Alamgir the winner.

Every presidential election since the country became a parliamentary democracy in October 1991 had been uncontested.

The post fell vacant last month following the resignation of the former president, Mohammed Shahabuddin, who stepped down for health reasons. The former president was a close ally of ousted Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina.

Alamgir has served as the BNP's secretary-general since 2016 and as minister of local government before the party returned to power in the February 2026 election. After receiving the party's nomination last week, he stepped down as secretary-general and resigned from the party's National Standing Committee.

The president's role in Bangladesh is largely constitutional and ceremonial, while executive authority rests with the government headed by Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, who was sworn in on February 17.

Alamgir took the oath of office on Friday at the Darbar Hall of Bangabhaban, the presidential palace. His election has reopened debate over the party whip system under Article 70 of the constitution.
