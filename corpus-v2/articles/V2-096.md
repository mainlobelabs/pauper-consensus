Syria says it has arrested intelligence officer over 2013 Tadamon massacre
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 24, 2026 (via Al Jazeera, AP)

Syrian authorities have announced the arrest of Amjad Youssef, an intelligence officer of the Syrian Arab Armed Forces, for his role in the Tadamon massacre during the civil war. On Friday, April 24, 2026, the interior ministry of the transitional government led by Ahmed al-Sharaa announced that Youssef, 40, was captured by the Syrian transitional government. Interior minister Anas Khattab said he was in custody.

Youssef had been in charge of security operations in south Damascus in the Assad era. He was captured in the Al-Ghab Plain of Hama province, about 50 km away and roughly 200 km from Tadamon, after days of surveillance and a "carefully executed security operation." Footage of the arrest shows him handcuffed with a bloodied face, and Khattab was filmed questioning him, asking, "Don't you have children?"

Youssef is the main suspect in the Tadamon massacre of April 16, 2013, in which 41 people were documented killed near the Othman Mosque and a pit in a Human Rights Watch site visit, with an estimated 288 total killed, including 12 children. Videos of the killers' own footage leaked in 2022, and the site is known locally as "Amjad Yousef's Pit." His friend Ahmed al-Harmouni was arrested in Germany in 2023.

Youssef had fled to Lebanon and then to Europe, where he reportedly underwent cosmetic surgery. Victims' families celebrated in the streets when the arrest was announced, and US envoy Tom Barrack welcomed the move as a step toward accountability.
