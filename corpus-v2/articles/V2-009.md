South Korea sentences ex-president Yoon Suk Yeol to life in prison
Wikipedia Current events portal, February 19, 2026 (via JURIST, Al Jazeera)

A South Korean court has sentenced former president Yoon Suk Yeol to life in prison for leading an insurrection when he declared martial law on December 3, 2024.

The Seoul Central District Court delivered the sentence on Thursday, February 19, 2026. It is the harshest punishment yet imposed on the former president, whose case has dominated South Korean politics for a year. The court found that Yoon acted with the "intent to subvert the Constitution" in ordering the brief martial law declaration. The prosecution had sought the death penalty.

Yoon declared martial law from the presidential office on the evening of December 3, 2024, and the National Assembly voted to lift it within hours. The decree triggered his impeachment, a suspension by the Constitutional Court, and a chain of criminal proceedings that continued through 2026.

The verdict marks the culmination of a year of legal proceedings against the former president. Yoon receives the life sentence in a case separate from an earlier five-year prison term imposed on January 16, 2026, for obstruction of justice and abuse of power, a sentence that was later raised to seven years. The two cases together reflect the breadth of the charges brought against him.
