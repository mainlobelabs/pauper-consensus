Radev's Progressive Bulgaria wins absolute majority in Bulgaria's eighth election since 2021
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 20, 2026 (via Al Jazeera, BTA)

Preliminary results from Bulgaria's eighth election since April 2021 reveal that the left-wing nationalist and populist Progressive Bulgaria coalition, led by former president Rumen Radev, has secured a majority of the 240 seats in the National Assembly. The vote took place on Sunday, April 19, 2026, after the December 2025 mass protests toppled the Zhelyazkov government, and Radev resigned the presidency in January 2026 to run.

With 98.3 to 100 percent of ballots counted, Progressive Bulgaria won 44.6 to 44.7 percent, giving it roughly 130 to 132 seats, the first absolute majority for a Bulgarian party since 1997. GERB-SDS, led by Boyko Borisov, won 13.4 percent, its worst result ever, PP-DB took 12.6 to 12.8 percent, DPS/MRF won between five and 6.8 percent, and Revival squeaked in at 4.3 percent.

The Socialist Party fell below the four percent threshold for the first time since 1990, and only five parties entered parliament, compared with nine in 2024. Turnout was about 50 percent, the highest since 2017, and Radev, calling the result "a victory of hope over distrust, freedom over fear," is set to become prime minister.

The repeated elections since 2021 have left Bulgaria in a prolonged political crisis, with governments collapsing before completing their terms, and the result gives the coalition a mandate to govern without a junior partner for the first time in years.
