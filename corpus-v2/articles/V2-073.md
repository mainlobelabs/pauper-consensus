China and Russia veto UN Security Council resolution on Strait of Hormuz
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 7, 2026 (via Reuters, UN News)

China and Russia have vetoed a United Nations Security Council resolution calling on UN member states to coordinate defensive efforts to ensure the safety of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz. On Tuesday, April 7, 2026, the two countries blocked the Bahrain-sponsored draft, saying it was biased against Iran; Bahrain held the rotating presidency. The vote was 11 in favor, 2 against, and 2 abstentions (Pakistan and Colombia).

The final text "strongly encourages" states to "coordinate efforts, defensive in nature" for safe navigation, including escorts, and demands that Iran halt its attacks on shipping. The original draft had contained the phrase "all necessary means" under Chapter 7, but was watered down after opposition from France, China, and Russia.

Russian ambassador Nebenzya said US and Israeli attacks were "not mentioned at all," and China's Fu Cong said the text "failed to capture the root causes." The vote came hours before US president Donald Trump's deadline, in which he said "whole civilization will die tonight," and which he withdrew the same day.

US envoy Waltz described the veto as "a new low." The strait carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, and its closure has been a central economic pressure point of the war, with the resolution seen as a Western attempt to keep navigation open.
