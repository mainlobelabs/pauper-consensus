Colombia earthquake toll rises to over 240 dead as state of emergency declared
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 11, 2026 (via BBC, Al Jazeera, EFE, Wikipedia)

The death toll from a magnitude-7.4 earthquake in Colombia has risen to more than 240 killed, 570 injured, and at least 3,000 still missing, and president Abelardo de la Espriella has declared a national state of emergency.

The quake struck at 7:34 local time on Saturday, 10 August, with its epicentre near San Jose del Palmar in Choco department, at a depth of roughly 110 kilometres. It was the strongest earthquake recorded in Colombia this century and was felt across all 32 departments of the country, as well as in Ecuador, Panama, and Venezuela.

The worst damage was reported in Cali, Pereira, Quibdo, and Manizales, where buildings collapsed and roads were cut off. De la Espriella set up a unified command post, deployed more than 1,000 troops to Cali, and imposed curfews in Cali and Pereira. Damage is estimated at $9.6 billion.

The government said search-and-rescue operations continue, with thousands of rescuers and international assistance on the ground. Authorities have warned that the death toll is likely to rise as the search for the missing continues in the remote areas of the Pacific coast, where access remains difficult.
