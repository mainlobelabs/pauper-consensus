UN office in Haiti reports 613 killed, 375 injured amid gang violence since March
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 6, 2026 (via BINUH, OHCHR, ReliefWeb)

The UN Integrated Office in Haiti, BINUH, has reported that at least 613 people were killed and 375 others injured between 6 March and 31 July 2026, amid intensifying gang violence across the country.

In its flash report, the office said the Plaine du Cul-de-Sac and several Cité Soleil neighborhoods saw a resurgence of armed violence from March, as the Viv Ansanm gang coalition reconfigured and weakened amid increased security-force pressure elsewhere in the capital. The fighting unfolded in four phases, and several documented acts may amount to serious violations of international law.

The report found that 64 percent of those killed or injured were gang-associated, with about a third unaffiliated, hit by stray bullets or killed on suspicion. Some 90 percent of casualties resulted from gang violence, about 9 percent from security-force operations, and 1 percent from mob violence.

The office documented more than 176 women and girls, aged 10 to 71, raped, including gang rapes used as punishment, and said the figure is likely underreported.

In its previous report of 8 May, covering January to March, BINUH had recorded 1,642 killed and 745 injured. The UN said the violence has left hundreds of thousands displaced and is straining the health system, and it called for sustained international support for the security forces and for protection of civilians.
