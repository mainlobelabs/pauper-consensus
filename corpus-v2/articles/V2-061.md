UK regulator orders lenders to compensate 12 million car finance customers
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 30, 2026 (via BBC, FCA)

The UK's Financial Conduct Authority has ruled that around 12 million people are entitled to compensation for mis-sold motor finance agreements made between 2014 and 2024, at an average of about 829 pounds each.

The redress scheme, published on Monday, March 30, 2026, is set to cost car finance lenders a total of 9.1 billion pounds. The FCA determined that the interest-rate structures in the affected agreements were unfair to borrowers, and it ordered the lenders to make payments to affected customers.

The regulator said that millions of customers would be compensated in 2026, with most of the payments completed by the end of 2027. The scheme has since been challenged in the courts, with the Upper Tribunal suspending parts of it in July 2026.

Car finance is one of the largest markets in the UK's consumer credit sector, with tens of millions of active agreements. The order follows the FCA's review of the interest-rate structures used in a large share of car finance products, which the regulator found to have been set at a level that was unfair to borrowers, and it is one of the biggest consumer compensation orders in the UK's recent history.
