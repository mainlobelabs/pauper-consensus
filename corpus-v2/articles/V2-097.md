Gunman detained at White House Correspondents' Dinner; Trump and Vance evacuated unharmed
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 25, 2026 (via NBC, AP)

Gunshots were fired during the White House Correspondents' Dinner at the Washington Hilton, and president Donald Trump, first lady Melania Trump, and vice president JD Vance were evacuated unharmed, while a 31-year-old man was detained as the suspect. The incident occurred in the ballroom on Saturday, April 25, 2026, at about 8:34 to 8:36 p.m., during the first such dinner for Trump as president.

Cole Tomas Allen, 31, of Torrance, California, a teacher and engineer, armed with a shotgun, a handgun, and multiple knives, rushed a security checkpoint, sprinted toward the ballroom, and exchanged gunfire before being tackled and handcuffed. He was not shot and has been hospitalized. One Secret Service officer of the Uniformed Division was hit in the vest at very close range, but was released, and officials said "the vest did the job." No one else was injured.

Vance was offstage in about four seconds, and Trump in about 20 seconds. Defense secretary Hegseth, secretary of state Rubio, and other senior officials, including Leavitt, Miller, and Scavino, were also present, and guests ducked under tables. Allen is believed to have acted alone; he was a hotel guest who traveled by train from Los Angeles to Chicago and then to Washington, and he checked in within the last day or two.

About 10 minutes before the incident, Allen sent a "manifesto" to his family that was anti-Trump and targeted administration officials. He faces three initial charges, including an attempt to assassinate the president, which carries up to life in prison, plus two counts of a firearm during a crime of violence and an assault on a federal officer. The dinner was canceled and is to be rescheduled within 30 days.
