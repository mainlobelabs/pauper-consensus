Brazilian Senate rejects Messias's Supreme Court nomination in first such defeat in 132 years
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 29, 2026 (via Reuters, Folha)

The Brazilian federal senate has rejected attorney general Jorge Messias's nomination to the Supreme Federal Court in a 42-34 secret ballot, the first such rejection in more than a century. On Wednesday, April 29, 2026, the full Senate voted 42 against and 34 for, with one abstention and four absences, and 41 of 81 votes were required for approval.

Messias, 46, has served as attorney general since 2023 and is the third nominee of president Lula's term. He was nominated in November 2025 for the seat vacated by the retirement of Justice Luis Roberto Barroso. The rejection is the first of a presidential STF nominee in 132 years, the previous blocks having occurred in 1894 under president Floriano Peixoto, who blocked five nominees, and it is the first under the 1988 constitution.

The defeat was driven by Senate president Davi Alcolumbre of União Brasil, who preferred Rodrigo Pacheco and blocked the formal submission of the nomination until April 1; the process ran a record 160 days, the longest ever, after five months of lobbying. The CCJ committee had approved the nomination earlier the same day by 16-11, after an eight-hour hearing.

Messias said "the Senate is sovereign and has spoken," and Lula is expected to name a new nominee, for which there is no constitutional deadline. The rejection is a major setback for the government in a branch of the state where its standing has been contested.
