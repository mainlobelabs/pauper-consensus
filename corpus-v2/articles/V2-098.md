Bennett and Lapid merge parties into Together in bid to unseat Netanyahu
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 26, 2026 (via Times of Israel, Haaretz)

Former Israeli prime ministers Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid have merged their parties into a new united list called Together, with space left open for Yashar chair Gadi Eisenkot, in an effort to oust prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu in the upcoming elections. At a press conference in Herzliya on Sunday, April 26, 2026, the two announced the merger of Yesh Atid, led by Lapid, 62, the opposition leader, and Bennett 2026, led by Bennett, 54.

The agreement, signed Saturday night after about a week of talks, creates a united list called "Together - Led by Bennett," with Bennett heading the slate and serving as the prime minister candidate. If Eisenkot, a former IDF chief of staff, joins, the leadership order will be Bennett, Eisenkot, Lapid. Bennett said "our door is open for you too" and called the merger "the most Zionist and patriotic act we have ever done... the era of division is over." Eisenkot welcomed the move but did not commit, saying "the door is definitely open."

The goal is to unite the opposition to oust Netanyahu in the Knesset election set for October 27, 2026, which can be brought forward with 90 days' notice. Bennett and Lapid previously co-led the 2021-22 Change government, which lasted 18 months with Ra'am and was the first interruption of Netanyahu's rule since 2009. Bennett cited the Hungarian model, where Péter Magyar's Tisza swept out Viktor Orbán.

Polls put Bennett's list at 21 to 24 percent, neck and neck with Likud at 24 to 25 percent, Lapid's list at seven percent, and Yashar at 12 percent, and the merged leaders said there would be no future government with the Arab or Haredi parties. Likud poked at the merger with an AI image of Mansour Abbas driving with the two in the backseat, captioned "Mansour is the driver," and deputy foreign minister Deri called it a "brotherhood of hatred."
