Astronomers announce discovery of the first black hole star, MoM-BH*-1, in Cetus
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 12, 2026 (via MIT News, The Guardian, space.com)

Astronomers studying a little red dot discovered by the James Webb Space Telescope have announced the discovery of the first black hole star, MoM-BH*-1, in the constellation Cetus, in the journal Nature.

The object is the size of the Solar System and emits light that outshines its host galaxy. The team proposed that MoM-BH*-1 is a black hole star, also known as a quasi-star, made up of a central black hole surrounded by a large, dense gas cloud. It is the first object ever identified as one.

The telescope spotted the bright red dot in the very early universe, about 660 million years after the Big Bang. The object's brightness, far too high for its size under standard stellar models, is consistent with a black hole at its core heating the surrounding gas.

The discovery is framed as strong evidence that black hole stars lie at the heart of the JWST's mysterious little red dots, a class of objects found in the early universe that have defied explanation. MoM-BH*-1 is the brightest red dot spotted so far.

The result could help resolve one of the telescope's major puzzles and test models of how the first massive black holes formed, the team said. It was reported by MIT News, the Guardian, and space.com.
