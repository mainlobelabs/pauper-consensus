HPV vaccine has prevented around 200 cervical cancer deaths in England, study finds
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 18, 2026 (via The Lancet, QMUL, Independent)

A study published in The Lancet, led by Queen Mary University of London and funded by Cancer Research UK, estimates that the HPV vaccine has prevented around 200 deaths from cervical cancer in England and finds that no women aged 20-24 died from the disease between 2020 and 2024.

The analysis, led by cancer epidemiologist Professor Peter Sasieni, covered mortality data from 2001 to 2024 and linked England's national vaccination programme, launched in 2008, with a reduction of about 200 cervical cancer deaths up to the end of 2024. For women aged 20-24 in 2020-24, the study found zero deaths against 23.1 expected, a 100% mortality reduction and the first such result in England.

Earlier cohorts showed an 80% mortality reduction for women aged 20-24 in 2015-19 and 69% for women aged 25-29 in 2020-24. The authors described the risk of dying before 30 in the vaccinated cohorts as "near-zero" and said the benefit is expected to rise as the cohorts age.

Cancer Research UK called the findings an "incredible milestone." The authors also cautioned that vaccine uptake is falling and called for sustained investment and catch-up programmes. It is the first study to show the vaccine's impact on cervical cancer mortality.
