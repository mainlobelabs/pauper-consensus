Colombia's Special Jurisdiction for Peace charges 27 former FARC leaders
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 4, 2026 (via EFE, JEP, Wikipedia)

Colombia's Special Jurisdiction for Peace, the transitional-justice tribunal of the 2016 peace agreements, has charged 27 former commanders of the now-defunct FARC-EP's Southern and Eastern blocks with war crimes and crimes against humanity committed during the conflict.

The imputation, filed this Tuesday, includes four former members of the guerrilla's final secretariat, among them Timoleón Jiménez, known as Timochenko, who served as FARC's supreme commander. The charged crimes include forced disappearances, massacres, forced displacements, torture, and rape across 15 cases, with 2,436 victims.

This is the first imputation in the JEP's macro-cases 10 and 11, which cover the non-amnestiable crimes and gender-based violence of the former guerrilla. Under the 2016 agreements, the JEP can hand down restorative sanctions of up to 20 years instead of prison terms for those who fully confess and make reparation.

In its first round of sanctions in September 2025, the tribunal sentenced seven ex-commanders to eight-year restorative measures for the mass kidnapping of more than 21,000 civilians.

The charges come amid growing pressure on the JEP from the government of president Abelardo de la Espriella, which has questioned the tribunal's pace. Human rights groups welcomed the imputation as a step toward accountability for the decades-long armed conflict.
