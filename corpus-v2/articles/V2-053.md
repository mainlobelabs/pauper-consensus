Italians reject reform to select judges by lot
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 23, 2026 (via JURIST, BBC)

Voters in Italy have rejected a constitutional amendment that would have selected members of the High Council of the Judiciary through sortition, with roughly 54 percent voting against and 46 percent in favor.

The referendum, held on March 22 and 23, 2026, delivered a clear rejection of the so-called "Nordio reform," named for the government's minister of justice, Carlo Nordio. The package would have separated judges and prosecutors, split the High Council into two bodies, selected some members by lot, and created a new disciplinary court.

The result is a significant blow to the Meloni government, which had championed the reform as a response to years of public concern about the judiciary. The reform had been one of the government's signature domestic initiatives, and its failure is expected to affect the government's standing in parliament.

The High Council of the Judiciary governs the careers, promotions and discipline of Italy's judges and prosecutors, and it has long been a point of contention in Italian politics. The vote is the latest in a series of referendums used to settle disputes between the government and the judiciary, and turnout was high in the north of the country, where support for the reform was strongest.
