Iran confirms two-week ceasefire with the US, including reopening of the Strait of Hormuz
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 8, 2026 (via BBC, Al Jazeera)

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi has confirmed the two-week ceasefire between Iran and the United States, which includes reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The agreement was announced at 6:32 p.m. EDT on Tuesday, April 7, and it took effect on Wednesday, April 8, 2026, when Iran's Supreme National Security Council ratified the ceasefire.

US president Donald Trump suspended "the bombing and attack of Iran for a period of two weeks," subject to "COMPLETE, IMMEDIATE, and SAFE OPENING of the Strait of Hormuz." The ceasefire was brokered by Pakistan, with prime minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir mediating, and Iran's 10-point plan was described as a "workable basis."

Araghchi said safe passage through the strait would last 14 days and would be coordinated with the Iranian armed forces, and that "if attacks against Iran are halted, our Powerful Armed Forces will cease their defensive operations." Talks between the two sides were set to begin in Islamabad on Friday, April 10. Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel supported the ceasefire subject to conditions.

Iran's foreign ministry stated that the ceasefire "does not signify the termination of the war." The agreement is the first formal pause in hostilities since the war began on February 28.
