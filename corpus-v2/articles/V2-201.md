Astronomers directly image exoplanet Beta Pictoris d with JWST and the VLT
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 15, 2026 (via ESO, IOP, phys.org)

Astronomers have directly imaged the exoplanet Beta Pictoris d next to its star, located 63 light-years away, using NASA's James Webb Space Telescope and the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope. The discovery was published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters on 15 July.

Two independent, simultaneous discoveries reported the third planet in the Beta Pictoris system. One team, led by Sutlieff, BONSE, and Birkby, first detected the planet on 3 December 2025 with the VLT's ERIS instrument and confirmed it across an 11-year archival baseline that included JWST/NIRCam and VLT/SPHERE observations, describing "a decade-long game of hide-and-seek" while the planet hid near the larger planet Beta Pictoris b. A second team from the University of California made a serendipitous detection in JWST/NIRSpec integral field unit data from 28 November 2025 and confirmed it with MIRI follow-ups.

The planet has a mass of about 2.4 times Jupiter, a temperature of about 600 kelvin, and orbits at about 26 astronomical units. It is roughly 100 times fainter than Beta Pictoris b, making it among the faintest exoplanets ever imaged directly from the ground, and only the second exoplanet with a likely mid-T spectral type. Beta Pictoris is now the second directly imaged system with three or more confirmed planets.
