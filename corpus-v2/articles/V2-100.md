Sabastian Sawe runs the first official sub-two-hour marathon, 1:59:30 world record in London
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 26, 2026 (via World Athletics, BBC Sport)

Sabastian Sawe and Yomif Kejelcha have become the first people to run a marathon in under two hours, with Sawe setting a new world record of 1:59:30 at the TCS London Marathon. On Sunday, April 26, 2026, at the dry and sunny Platinum Label road race, Sawe, 31, of Kenya, the defending champion, finished in 1:59:30, the first competitive, official sub-two-hour marathon in history.

Sawe's time passes Eliud Kipchoge's 1:59:40.2 set in a 2019 Vienna exhibition, which was not an official race, and is 65 seconds inside the previous world record of 2:00:35 set by Kelvin Kiptum at the 2023 Chicago Marathon. Sawe split the halves in 60:29 and 59:01, a negative split, and broke clear of Kejelcha in the final mile. Kejelcha, 28, of Ethiopia, a marathon debutant who is a twice 10,000m world silver medalist and indoor mile world record holder, finished in 1:59:41, the second-fastest time ever and the fastest marathon debut in history.

Kiplimo of Uganda was third in 2:00:28, leaving three men inside the previous world record. In the women's race, Tigst Assefa of Ethiopia retained her title in 2:15:41, improving her own separate-field women-only world record by nine seconds, and it was the first time three women finished under 2:16, with Obiri at 2:15:53 and Jepkosgei at 2:15:55.

Hassan and Jepchirchir withdrew from the women's race before the start. The sub-two-hour barrier had long been the most famous target in distance running, and it had been crossed only in non-race exhibitions until the barrier fell in an official race in London.
