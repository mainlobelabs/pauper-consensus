NASA launches Artemis II for first crewed lunar flyby since Apollo
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 1, 2026 (via NASA, AP)

NASA has launched Artemis II from the Kennedy Space Center in Florida, sending four astronauts on the first crewed lunar flyby mission since Apollo 17 in 1972. The Space Launch System rocket lifted off from Pad 39B at 6:35 p.m. EDT on Wednesday, April 1, 2026.

The crew of the Orion spacecraft, which the astronauts named Integrity, includes Reid Wiseman as commander, Victor Glover as pilot, Christina Koch as mission specialist, and Canadian astronaut Jeremy Hansen, a rookie on the mission and the first Canadian to travel beyond low Earth orbit. The roughly 10-day mission, lasting 9 days 1 hour 32 minutes, follows a free-return trajectory, and the spacecraft performs a lunar flyby of about 7 hours on Monday, April 6, reaching roughly 252,756 to 252,799 miles from Earth, the farthest humans have ever traveled.

During the flyby, the crew will pass over the far side of the Moon, a region never seen by humans, and a total solar eclipse will be visible from the ship. The crew named a crater on the Moon Carroll in honor of Wiseman's late wife.

Splashdown in the Pacific off San Diego is scheduled for April 10. About 400,000 spectators watched the launch along the Space Coast, and the spent boosters are marked to honor the 250th anniversary of the United States. The mission is a key step in NASA's program to return humans to the vicinity of the Moon.
