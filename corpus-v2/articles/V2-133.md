Paris Court of Appeal convicts Airbus and Air France of manslaughter in 2009 AF447 crash
Wikipedia Current events portal, May 21, 2026 (via BBC, Bloomberg, France24)

The Court of Appeal of Paris on Thursday, May 21, 2026, convicted Airbus and Air France of manslaughter over the 2009 crash of Air France Flight 447, which killed 228 people, and ordered both companies to pay the maximum corporate fine of 225,000 euros each. The flight, an A330 bound from Rio de Janeiro to Paris, crashed on June 1, 2009, with 216 passengers and 12 crew aboard, in France's worst aviation disaster.

The court found both companies solely and entirely responsible, and guilty on all 20 manslaughter counts, ruling that Air France failed to train pilots for pitot-tube icing scenarios and to adequately inform crews. The verdict overturns a 2023 lower-court acquittal that had blamed pilot error and was a major setback for the families, following an eight-week appeal trial.

Both companies, which had blamed the pilots, said they would appeal to the Court of Cassation, calling the fines a fraction of their revenue. The victims' association, whose president lost his son on the flight, said justice had been done, and lawyers for the families said a second full re-trial cannot be ruled out.
