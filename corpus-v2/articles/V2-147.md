Philippine senator Estrada and former public works secretary Bonoan surrender to police on plunder charges
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 1, 2026 (via GMA, Rappler, Philstar, Inquirer)

Philippine senator Jinggoy Estrada and former public works secretary Manuel Bonoan surrender to police after an anti-graft court orders their arrest on plunder charges related to an alleged 573-million peso (US$9.3 million) corruption scheme involving flood control projects.

The Sandiganbayan anti-graft court issued non-bailable plunder warrants for Estrada, Bonoan, and three former Department of Public Works and Highways engineers. The Ombudsman's office charged the men on 28 May with illegal budgetary insertions and project allocations in the department's 2025 infrastructure portfolio, in which public funds were funneled into designated projects in exchange for predetermined commissions and kickbacks.

Estrada and Bonoan surrendered at the Philippine National Police Criminal Investigation and Detection Group in Quezon City. Estrada denied the allegations, called them politically motivated, and said, "I will not be broken by accusations." The interior minister, who personally led the warrant service, insisted Estrada was formally arrested and had his rights read.

The two were held at the New Quezon City Jail, a designated flood-control detention facility that also held former senator Bong Revilla. Bonoan was placed under hospital arrest after his blood pressure spiked to 192/100 during booking. Their arraignment begins on 2 June. Estrada is the highest-ranking sitting official arraigned in the scandal.
