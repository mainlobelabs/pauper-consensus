Somalia's federal army takes Baidoa in clash with South West state
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 30, 2026 (via Al Jazeera, ABC)

Somalia's national army has taken control of Baidoa, the capital of the South West state, in a clash that has killed at least 10 people and displaced more than 45,000, two weeks after the state government severed ties with the federal government.

The fighting erupted on Monday, March 30, 2026, between federal forces and militias loyal to South West state president Abdiaziz Laftagareen, who announced his resignation in the wake of the clashes. A hospital official put the number of injured at about 98, including at least four civilians among the dead.

The clash followed Laftagareen's government severing ties with the federal government in Mogadishu roughly two weeks earlier. The federal government said the operation was necessary to restore the authority of the state, and it called on residents to avoid the areas of fighting.

Baidoa has served as Somalia's temporary capital in earlier periods of the country's history, and the city's capture is a significant step in the confrontation between the federal government and the South West state. Somalia's federal system divides power between the central government and the regional states, and the breakdown in relations had raised fears of a wider crisis.
