Lafarge and former executives convicted in Paris for financing Islamic State in Syria
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 13, 2026 (via BBC, Guardian)

A court in Paris has convicted cement company Lafarge and several former executives, including former CEO Bruno Lafont, for financing the Islamic State and other Syrian armed groups between 2013 and 2014. On Monday, April 13, 2026, the Paris Criminal Court found the company, now owned by Holcim, guilty of financing terrorism and of breaching EU sanctions.

The company paid a total of EUR 5.59 million between 2013 and September 2014 to the Islamic State, the al-Nusra Front, and Ahrar al-Sham to keep its cement plant at Jalabiya running. The plant, which Lafarge bought in 2008 for USD 680 million, had been operating since 2010 in the middle of Syria's civil war.

The court imposed a maximum fine of EUR 1.125 million, a sanctions fine of EUR 4.57 million shared with four former executives, and EUR 30 million in asset forfeiture. Eight former employees were found guilty: CEO Bruno Lafont received six years and a EUR 225,000 fine, deputy COO Christian Herrault received five years, and intermediary Firas Tlass received seven years in absentia, with the others receiving sentences of one to seven years. Lafont was jailed immediately and is appealing.

Lafarge is the first company tried in France for financing terrorism. The verdict follows a 2022 French conviction for crimes against humanity and a 2022 US plea deal of USD 778 million over the same facts.
