UK government to repeal the 202-year-old Vagrancy Act, ending the criminalisation of rough sleeping
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 28, 2026 (via gov.uk, Independent, Crisis UK)

The British government announces that it will scrap the 202-year-old Vagrancy Act that criminalizes rough sleeping in England and Wales, with a new law aiming at preventing homelessness rather than punishing it.

Housing secretary Steve Reed and MP Alison McGovern said on Thursday, 28 June 2026, that the government will repeal the Vagrancy Act on Monday, 29 June 2026, "ending nearly two centuries of legislation that has criminalised rough sleeping and begging." The Vagrancy Act, from 1824 in the era of the Napoleonic Wars and the Industrial Revolution, targeted "idle and disorderly persons, rogues and vagabonds."

The repeal takes effect through secondary legislation under the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022. Reed said the shift is "from punishment to prevention," and that "Homeless people are not criminals, they are people who need help."

New offences in the Crime and Policing Act 2026, which passed in April, fill the gap, targeting organised begging gangs, facilitating begging for gain, and trespass with intent to commit crime. The measure is part of a £3.6bn National Plan to End Homelessness that aims to halve long-term rough sleeping in three years.

Repeal was first signalled by the Conservatives in 2022, and Labour pledged it for spring 2026. Charities including Crisis, the Big Issue, and St Mungo's called it a "watershed" and "defining moment." Rough sleeping in Wales is decriminalised the same day.
