Armenia's Civil Contract wins election majority but falls short of the referendum threshold
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 8, 2026 (via BBC, Reuters, AP)

Preliminary results from Armenia's first National Assembly election since the expulsion of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians in late 2023 show the governing Civil Contract party won 49.8% of votes, a parliamentary majority but short of the two-thirds needed for a constitutional referendum on a peace deal with Azerbaijan; opposition alliances Strong Armenia and Armenia Alliance won 23.2% and 9.9% respectively.

The preliminary Central Electoral Commission results, covering all 2,005 polling stations, were released on Monday, 8 June 2026, after voting on Sunday. Turnout was about 59%.

Civil Contract, Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan's centrist, pro-Western party, will take 64 of 105 seats, enough to form a government but six short of the 70 needed to call a constitutional referendum on the Azerbaijan peace deal. The election was seen as a test of Pashinyan's turn to the West and the path toward EU accession.

International observers, including OSCE and European Union monitors, alleged Russian interference and pressure. Strong Armenia appealed to the electoral commission to annul the results, citing widespread violations, and opposition supporters rallied outside the commission's building.

The two pro-Russian alliances, one led by former president Robert Kocharyan, combined for more than 31% of the vote, a better-than-expected result as Pashinyan's approval has fallen from 54% in 2021 to about 30% in 2026.
