South Korean court sentences ex-justice minister Park Sung-jae to 25 years for martial law role
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 22, 2026 (via AP, Yonhap, Korea Herald)

A South Korean court sentences former justice minister Park Sung-jae to 25 years in prison for involvement in former president Yoon Suk Yeol's martial law declaration.

The Seoul Central District Court found Park guilty on Monday, 22 June 2026, of a key role in the insurrection and abuse of power. After the martial law declaration of 3 December 2024, he convened an emergency meeting of senior justice officials, reviewed dispatching prosecutors to the martial-law joint investigation headquarters, and checked correctional facility detention capacities to "hold politicians and key figures."

The court said Yoon's declaration, its enforcement, and attempts to occupy the National Assembly and the National Election Commission were "a riot aimed at subverting the constitutional order," and that Park, who "recognized the unconstitutionality and illegality," acted "for the purpose of subverting the Constitution" and "ultimately turned his back on his duty of upholding the Constitution at the idea that the insurrection could succeed, choosing to instead take part in it."

The sentence is five years more than the 20 years sought by special counsel Cho Eun-suk. Park was immediately remanded, and the anti-graft charge was dismissed as outside the special counsel's mandate.

Park joins former prime minister Han Duck-soo and former defence minister Kim Yong-hyun among convicted cabinet members, and he has filed an immediate appeal.
