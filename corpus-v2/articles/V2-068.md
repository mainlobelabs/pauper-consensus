Pakistan delivers ceasefire plan for Iran war, dubbed the Islamabad Accord
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 6, 2026 (via Reuters, Axios)

Pakistan has proposed a ceasefire plan for the war between Iran and the United States, which was received by both sides overnight Sunday, April 5, and Monday, April 6, 2026, and has been dubbed the Islamabad Accord. The plan calls for an immediate halt to hostilities and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, followed by a 15- to 20-day period of negotiations between Iran and the United States.

The two-tier plan envisions a regional framework for the strait and final in-person talks in Islamabad, with the final deal to see Iran give up nuclear weapons in exchange for sanctions relief and the release of frozen assets. The memorandum of understanding is to be finalized electronically through Pakistan, which has served as the sole communication channel between the two sides.

Pakistani army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir has been in contact "all night long" with US vice president JD Vance, US envoy Steve Witkoff, and Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi. Iran initially refused to reopen the strait under a temporary ceasefire and set no deadline. Egypt, Turkey, and China have also been mediating, and Beijing's pressure helped move Tehran.

The proposal set the stage for a two-week ceasefire agreed on Wednesday, April 8. Subsequent talks in Islamabad on April 10-11 collapsed without a deal, with Vance reporting "bad news" and the United States citing Iran's failure to give a firm no-nuclear-weapons assurance, while Araghchi said the United States shifted the goalposts to a maximalist position at the last minute.
