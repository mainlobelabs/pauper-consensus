House passes DHS funding bill, ending 76-day agency shutdown; Trump signs
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 30, 2026 (via AP, The Hill)

The US House of Representatives has unanimously approved, by voice vote, a bill passed by the Senate to fund the Department of Homeland Security, the Secret Service, the Coast Guard, FEMA, TSA, and CISA, in an effort to end the 76-day government shutdown of the department. On Thursday, April 30, 2026, the House voted in under an hour after notice, with no recorded vote, to fund the agencies through the end of fiscal year 2026, on September 30.

The bill excludes ICE and parts of CBP, which are to be funded via a USD 70 billion reconciliation bill for the rest of Trump's term, to January 2029. The House GOP adopted a budget resolution on a party-line vote on Wednesday, and the ICE/CBP bill is expected in May. President Trump signed the measure Thursday afternoon, ending the 76-day DHS shutdown, the longest agency shutdown in US history; the prior record was a 43-day governmentwide closure last year.

The department had been unfunded since February 14, 2026, in the reckoning over Trump's deadly Minneapolis immigration crackdown. The Senate had passed the bill unanimously in a late-March overnight vote, but Speaker Johnson had held it in the House for more than five weeks, calling it "a joke." Senator Patty Murray said the hold "extended the DHS shutdown for over a month for no reason at all." Johnson reversed course on April 1, waited for the reconciliation process to start, and then moved the bill under Trump pressure before the Friday recess.

Leadership said it held "the homeland bill... to ensure they could not isolate and eliminate those two critical agencies." DHS secretary Markwayne Mullin, who replaced Noom mid-shutdown, warned that emergency payroll funds, redirected by a March executive order from Trump, would run out by early May, and airport security lines had grown in the meantime.
