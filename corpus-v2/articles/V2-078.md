Study finds 28 chimpanzees killed in ongoing war in Uganda's Kibale National Park
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 9, 2026 (via Science, NBC)

A study published in the journal Science on Thursday, April 9, 2026, finds that 28 chimpanzees, including 19 infants, have been killed during an ongoing chimpanzee war in Kibale National Park, Uganda. The paper, "Lethal conflict after group fission in wild chimpanzees," was led by researchers at the University of Texas at Austin and Arizona State University, and it appears in Science volume 392, pages 216-220.

The roughly 200-member Ngogo community polarized in 2015 and had split into Western and Central groups by 2018. Between 2018 and 2024, the Western group made 24 attacks, killing at least seven adult males and 17 infants. The paper describes the toll as a minimum, and press coverage cites the higher total as the conflict continues.

Such lethal conflicts between chimpanzee groups are rare events, occurring about once per 500 years, and had been observed only once before the Kibale war began. The park's long monitoring program, one of the most extensive in the world, is what made it possible to track the fighting over multiple years.

Because the conflict is ongoing, the total number of deaths is still rising. Researchers say the case is a rare window into the costs of territorial conflict in a species close to humans, and the findings add to evidence that chimpanzees wage organized, destructive wars comparable in character to some human conflicts.
