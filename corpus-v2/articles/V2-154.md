2025 had the most active state conflicts since World War II, Uppsala study finds
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 9, 2026 (via Uppsala University, PRIO, NPR)

A study published in the Journal of Peace Research using Uppsala Conflict Data Program data reports that 2025 saw 65 active conflicts, the highest number of conflicts between states since World War II and the highest number of fatalities since the Rwandan genocide.

Researchers at Uppsala University found that 65 state-based conflicts were active across 35 countries in 2025, the most since records began in 1946. Interstate conflicts doubled for the second year running, from two in 2023 to eight in 2025, including Russia-Ukraine, Iran-Israel, India-Pakistan, and Israel-Lebanon.

Thirteen conflicts were classified as wars by the threshold of at least 1,000 battle deaths per year, the most since 1992. About 244,600 people were killed in organized violence in 2025, up from about 188,000 in 2024, making it the second bloodiest year on record since the 1994 Rwandan genocide.

One-sided violence against civilians rose fivefold to more than 76,000 killed, the highest since the Rwandan genocide, driven by Sudan, including the El Fasher massacre in October 2025. The researchers said the trend predates the current US administration, citing Russia's Ukraine invasion, the Gaza bombardments, and the Iran-Israel war as key drivers.

Non-state violence fell to its lowest level since 2013. A companion PRIO study counted 245,000 battle-related deaths, the third-deadliest year since the Cold War behind 1994 and the 2021 Tigray war.
