UK's CMA clears Paramount Skydance's $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 6, 2026 (via The Guardian, FT, Reuters, Deadline)

The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has cleared Paramount Skydance's $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, removing one of the last major regulatory hurdles to the deal.

The CMA approved the transaction on Thursday, following a public interest intervention by culture secretary Lisa Nandy, who on 30 June had signalled her intention to intervene on public interest grounds, citing media freedom and on-demand programming.

Nandy stood down from the intervention after Paramount Skydance chief David Ellison agreed to a commitment on editorial independence. The UK antitrust trial is scheduled for March 2027.

The $110.9 billion asset purchase agreement was signed on 27 February 2026, after Paramount Skydance outbid Netflix in the bidding war. The US Department of Justice approved the deal on 12 June, and the European Commission approved it on 22 July, after Paramount agreed to divest its 50-50 stake in United International Pictures, the joint venture with Universal, within 13 months of closing.

Critics, including a group of British actors, argued that the merger threatens UK news independence and the funding of children's television. The deal would combine the back catalogues of two of Hollywood's oldest studios.
