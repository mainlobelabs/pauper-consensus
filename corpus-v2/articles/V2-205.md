Andy Burnham elected new leader of the British Labour Party
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 17, 2026 (via AP, Al Jazeera, BBC, labour.org.uk)

Andy Burnham has been elected as the new leader of the British Labour Party; he will formally succeed Keir Starmer as prime minister on Monday, 20 July, after Starmer tenders his resignation to King Charles III and the king invites Burnham to form a government.

Burnham, MP for Makerfield and former Greater Manchester mayor, was officially declared elected at Labour's special conference in London, announced by NEC chair Shabana Mahmood. He received nominations from 379 of the party's 403 MPs and from 23 affiliate organisations, including all 11 affiliated trade unions. The only other nominee, MP Catherine West, received a single vote. The ballot ran from 9 July to 15 July.

The contest followed Starmer's resignation as Labour leader on 22 June after the party's dismal May local results, Burnham's win in the Makerfield by-election, and a rebellion within the party.

Pledging to "bring back hope," Burnham said he would put the cost of living "front and centre," fight Reform UK, which Labour has trailed in polls for about 18 months, and eradicate factionalism in the party. A new cabinet is to be announced Monday. Burnham had run for the leadership twice before and lost both times.
