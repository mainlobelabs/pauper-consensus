Man sentenced to life with 21-year minimum for murder of University of Southampton student Henry Nowak
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 1, 2026 (via BBC, The Guardian, Independent)

Vickrum Digwa, a 23-year-old Sikh man, is sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum of 21 years for murdering 18-year-old University of Southampton student Henry Nowak on 3 December 2025; his mother is also found guilty of assisting an offender by hiding the murder weapon, a knife.

Digwa was sentenced at Southampton Crown Court on Monday, 1 June 2026. The judge described a "wicked lie" in which Nowak, a first-year finance student from Essex and the first in his family to attend university, was handcuffed at the scene while still alive by misled officers after Digwa claimed Nowak had racially abused him and knocked his turban off.

Nowak was stabbed five times on Belmont Road in Southampton as he walked home alone after a night out. The weapon was a 21cm Sikh kirpan dagger carried for religious reasons. Digwa filmed Nowak's death in close-up and took his phone.

Digwa's mother, 53-year-old Kiran Kaur, took the knife from the scene and hid it at the family home. She is to be sentenced on 17 July. Digwa also received a concurrent two-year sentence for carrying a blade in a public place.

Nowak's family called the handcuffing "unbearable" and demanded a full, fearless and transparent investigation of the police response. Protests and police clashes broke out outside the court after the sentencing.
