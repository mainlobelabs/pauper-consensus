2026 FIFA World Cup opens in North America with the first 48-team field
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 11, 2026 (via FIFA, BBC, AP)

The 2026 FIFA World Cup, consisting of 48 national teams for the first time, begins with matches in Canada, Mexico, and the United States; an opening ceremony is held at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City.

Mexico beat South Africa 2-0 in the opening match at the Azteca on Thursday, 11 June 2026, making the stadium the first to host three World Cups after the 1970 and 1986 finals. Julian Quiñones opened the scoring after a steal by Erik Lira, and Raul Jiménez converted a penalty. Three players were sent off: two from South Africa and César Montes from Mexico.

At the ceremony, Shakira, performing at her fourth World Cup opening, sang the tournament anthem "Dai Dai" with Burna Boy. Maná, Andrea Bocelli, and Danny Ocean also performed, and Salma Hayek Pinault gave a surprise welcome. The event included a parade of all 48 flags and fireworks.

The tournament will play 104 matches across 16 venues in three countries, the largest World Cup ever. Canelo Álvarez was in the stands, and protests were also held at the stadium. Mexico, the 14th-ranked team, will play all three of its group matches at home, and the Canadian and American ceremonies follow on Friday.
