Syria sentences deposed president Bashar al-Assad and his brother Maher to death in absentia
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 11, 2026 (via The Guardian, SANA, Al Jazeera)

A criminal court in Damascus has sentenced deposed president Bashar al-Assad and his brother Maher to death in absentia, and former head of political security in Daraa, Atef Najib, to death in person, over premeditated murder and crimes against humanity.

The 4th Criminal Court of Syria's transitional government handed down death sentences to nine former regime officials. Former defence minister Fahd Jassem al-Freij and five other officials were also sentenced in absentia. Najib, Assad's first cousin, was the only defendant tried in person.

The court found the defendants guilty of the premeditated and deliberate murder of multiple people, including children, as well as torture, arbitrary detention, and crimes against humanity committed during the 14-year Syrian civil war, which killed about 500,000 people.

Najib was held responsible for the 2011 torture of 15 boys in Daraa after they painted an anti-Assad slogan on a school wall, an event widely seen as a spark of the revolution. Assad and Maher fled to Moscow in December 2024 after an 11-day rebel offensive toppled the regime.

The court said it would pursue international legal avenues to prosecute the two men, who remain outside Syria's reach, and the verdict is expected to deepen tensions with Russia, which has so far resisted extradition requests.
