UN: 922 migrants died or went missing on Red Sea route in 2025
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 25, 2026 (via Africanews, UN)

The International Organization for Migration has reported that 922 migrants died or went missing along the Eastern Route from the Horn of Africa to the Arabian Peninsula in 2025, the deadliest year on record for the route.

The figure, released by the UN agency on Wednesday, March 25, 2026, represents a significant increase from 2024 and is roughly double the previous year's toll. The agency said the rise reflects a higher number of departures and of the risks of the sea crossing.

The Eastern Route takes migrants from countries such as Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan across the Red Sea to Yemen and Saudi Arabia, often in small, overcrowded boats. The crossing has become one of the deadliest migration corridors in the world.

The IOM tracks migrant deaths and disappearances worldwide, and its annual data is the most comprehensive record of its kind. The report is expected to inform the agency's advocacy with governments along the route as conditions in the departure countries continue to drive people to make the crossing. The agency calls for the deaths to be treated as a matter of international concern, and it urges states to expand search-and-rescue coverage in the Red Sea.
