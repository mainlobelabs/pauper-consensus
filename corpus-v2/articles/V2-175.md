IMO announces an evacuation plan for ships stranded in the Persian Gulf
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 23, 2026 (via IMO, UN News, BBC)

The International Maritime Organization announces an evacuation plan for around 500 to 600 ships with 11,000 seafarers stranded in the Persian Gulf to sail through the Strait of Hormuz after the ceasefire deal, to be coordinated with Iran and Oman.

IMO secretary-general Arsenio Dominguez said on Tuesday, 23 June 2026, "We will begin the implementation of the evacuation plan for over 11,000 seafarers still stranded in the region," to be carried out in close cooperation with Iran, Oman, all other coastal states, the United States, and the maritime industry.

The plan provides two temporary sea lanes: a northern route in Iranian waters, coordinated by Iran, and a southern route in Omani and UAE waters, coordinated by Oman and the United States. The existing traffic separation scheme is unusable because of mines.

The approach is phased: vessels contacted through UKMTO are directed to a waiting area off Oman and are assigned to transit groups with specific departure days. The organization secured safety guarantees, including mine clearing.

At the peak, about 20,000 seafarers on roughly 3,200 vessels had been stranded since Iran halted traffic after the 28 February strikes. Fourteen seafarers have been killed in shipping attacks since the emergency began. Transits are expected to climb from the high 20s back to pre-war levels of about 130 per day.
