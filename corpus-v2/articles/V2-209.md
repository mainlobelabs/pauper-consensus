Skyroot Aerospace launches Vikram-1, India's first privately built orbital rocket
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 18, 2026 (via ISRO, SpaceNews, space.com, Times of India)

Skyroot Aerospace has successfully launched Vikram-1, India's first privately built orbital rocket, from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, carrying small satellite payloads into low Earth orbit on its maiden flight.

The 23-metre, three-stage solid rocket with a restartable liquid Orbit Adjustment Module lifted off at 12:05pm on 18 July from the first launch pad, the first private rocket launch from the pad. Skyroot said the flight was a complete success with all six payloads delivered. It carried six small payloads into orbit at about 450 kilometres and 60 degrees of inclination: Skyroot's SCOPE, Grahaa Space's SOLARAS S3 satellite, a DCUBED technology demo, Cosmoserve's Embrace debris-capture robotic arm, the "Cosmic Bloom" lab-grown-diamond artwork, and an 18-karat gold micro-rocket.

Skyroot, founded in 2018 by ex-ISRO scientists Pawan Kumar Chandana and Naga Bharath Daka, becomes the first private Indian company to reach orbit, on its first attempt. India becomes the third country, after the US and China, with private orbital launch capability.

Prime minister Narendra Modi made a phone call hailing the "self-reliance push" and "the dawn of a new space era." The company is targeting Vikram-2, with a 1,000 kilogram capacity, for 2027.
