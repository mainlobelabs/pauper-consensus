France authorizes culling of around 200 wolves
Wikipedia Current events portal, February 20, 2026 (via Reuters, RFI)

France's agriculture ministry has authorized the culling of around 200 wolves, raising the annual removal limit to 21 percent of the estimated national population, which exceeds 1,000 animals, with a possible increase to 23 percent.

The ministry cited rising livestock losses as wolves expand into farmland and areas near cities. The new limit applies to an estimated population of 1,082 wolves, and combined with a 2 percent option would allow up to 248 animals to be removed this year.

The change lifts the limit from 2025, when the figure stood at 19 percent of the population, corresponding to 190 animals. The decree authorizing the cull was pending at the time of reporting. The removals take place under France's national wolf management plan, which sets annual culling quotas by region. The ministry said the updated figures would be reviewed at the end of the culling season.

Farmers' organizations have long called for higher culling quotas to protect herds, while conservation groups oppose any significant removals, arguing that the wolf population remains small relative to historical levels.

Wolves have been returning to France from the Italian Alps since the 1970s, and their expanding range has increasingly brought them into contact with farmers and suburban residents.
