Flash floods and mudslides kill at least 160 in Nepal and Tibet along Trishuli River
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 26, 2026 (via AP, LA Times, BBC, Wikipedia)

Flash floods and mudslides along the Trishuli River have hit Nepal and Tibet. At least 160 people have been killed in Nepal, hundreds have been injured, and 463 others, including more than 200 tourists, are reported missing.

On the morning of 26 August 2026, a series of flash floods struck dozens of settlements in Nepal's Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts. The floods also hit the Gyirong Port area on the China-Nepal border, a key gateway for cross-border trade and tourism. Initial reports attributed the disaster to a glacier collapse and icefall, and seismologists said the signal was consistent with a landslide rather than an earthquake.

Chinese officials reported three deaths and 265 missing in Tibet, with Gyirong County and its port badly hit. A mudslide on the Nepal side caused significant casualties and missing persons at Gyirong Port, according to a CCTV report.

The upper Trishuli, also known as the Bhotekoshi, is fed by the Lhende Khola, which rises in Gyirong County. The disaster has cut off roads and damaged villages, and rescue operations have been hampered by continued rain and landslides.

Aid agencies have appealed for tents, water purification, and medical supplies, and both governments have dispatched army and rescue teams to the affected districts.
