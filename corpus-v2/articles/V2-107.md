Seoul High Court cuts ex-PM Han Duck-soo's sentence to 15 years for 2024 martial law role
Wikipedia Current events portal, May 7, 2026 (via Yonhap, AP)

The Seoul High Court reduced the prison sentence of former South Korean prime minister Han Duck-soo from 23 years to 15 on Thursday, May 7, 2026, for his role in the insurrection that aided former president Yoon Suk Yeol's martial law declaration on December 3, 2024, citing a lack of evidence that Han played a leading or premeditated role. The ruling was the first appellate decision among the cabinet members charged over the episode.

The court upheld the lower court's findings that Han created the appearance of a Cabinet deliberation, countersigned a backdated proclamation drafted by his aide to conceal legal flaws, and discussed plans with the interior minister to cut power and water to media outlets. It acquitted him on appeal of broad omission liability for failing to convene a proper Cabinet, of post-meeting countersignature-seeking, and of part of the perjury charge tied to his February 2025 impeachment-trial testimony.

Special counsel Cho Eun-suk had sought 23 years. The court cited Han's roughly 50 years of public service and found he did not conspire in advance or actively participate, noting he presided over the Cabinet after the National Assembly demanded the lifting. Han, 76, has been imprisoned since January and is expected to appeal to the Supreme Court.
