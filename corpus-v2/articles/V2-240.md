Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey sign the Mecca Joint Defence Pact
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 7, 2026 (via Reuters, Al Arabiya, France 24)

Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey have mutually signed the Mecca Joint Defence Pact, declaring that an attack on one of the three states will be considered an attack on all.

The trilateral pact was signed at the Makkah Al Mukarramah Joint Defense Summit, held at the Al-Safa Palace in Mecca at the invitation of Saudi king Salman. Pakistan's prime minister Shehbaz Sharif, Saudi crown prince and prime minister Mohammed bin Salman, and Turkish president Recep Tayyip Erdogan attended the signing.

The official joint statement says that any armed attack against any one of the three states shall be considered an attack on all of them, a clause modelled on NATO's Article 5. The pact builds on the bilateral Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, signed in Riyadh on 17 September 2025, which is now being expanded to include Turkey.

The signing comes amid the 2026 Iran War, in which Saudi Arabia has been struck by Iranian drones. Analysts described a division of roles: Saudi financial backing, Turkish defence technology, and Pakistani frontline manpower. The pact provides for NATO-style ministerial, political, and military committees, and a permanent secretariat based in Saudi Arabia.

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi welcomed the pact and called for Muslim unity, while India said it was following the development closely. Nationwide celebrations were reported in Pakistan.
