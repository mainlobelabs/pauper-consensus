Kraków voters back dismissal of mayor Miszalski in recall referendum; council vote invalid
Wikipedia Current events portal, May 24, 2026 (via Krakow.pl, TVP World, Politico)

Residents of Kraków, Poland, overwhelmingly backed the dismissal of mayor Aleksander Miszalski of Civic Platform and the city council in a local recall referendum on Sunday, May 24, 2026, for the first time in the city's postwar history. Exit polls showed 33.4 percent voter turnout, with 97.8 percent backing the mayor's dismissal and 96.0 percent backing removal of the council.

The referendum followed a public petition with more than 130,000 signatures, driven by disputes over the clean-transport zone, parking fees, and city debt, and was backed by PiS and Confederation. A 2025 law eased the recall thresholds. Miszalski, two years into a term won in 2024, had told his supporters not to vote in order to sink the turnout threshold, and he is expected to remain in office until the new mayoral election.

Official turnout of 29.99 percent cleared the mayoral threshold of 26.98 percent, making the dismissal decisive, with 171,581 votes for removal. A new mayoral election will be held within 90 days, possibly with a second round in September, with a commissioner appointed by the prime minister in the interim, while the council vote, 168,010 for removal against 6,713, failed its higher 30.59 percent threshold.
