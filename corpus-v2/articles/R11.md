Thailand approves early release of ex-PM Thaksin, who will serve the rest of his term on parole
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 29, 2026 (via AP, Bangkok Post)

The Thai corrections department has approved the early release of former prime minister Thaksin Shinawatra, who is serving a one-year prison sentence for corruption, citing his age and the limited time remaining on his sentence, with his release subject to probation conditions. On Wednesday, April 29, 2026, a Ministry of Justice parole panel, reviewing more than 500 eligible inmates nationwide, approved 858 of 859 applicants, including Thaksin.

Thaksin, 76, the 23rd prime minister, was granted parole from Klong Prem Central Prison and is to be released on Monday, May 11, 2026, after serving eight months, the two-thirds mark of his one-year term; his eligibility point was May 10. The panel's decision cited his age, over 70, underlying medical conditions, good behavior, low recidivism risk, and the limited time remaining on his sentence.

His release comes with a four-month probation, running until his sentence expires in September 2026, during which he must reside at his declared home in Ban Chan Song La, Bang Phlat, Bangkok, and report regularly to probation officials. The corrections department said an EM ankle bracelet would be required, though the Bangkok Post reported that no EM device would be required.

Thaksin was convicted in absentia of abuse of authority and conflict of interest, corruption-related offenses, returned to Thailand in 2023, and had his original eight-year sentence commuted by King Vajiralongkorn to one year, which he served at the Police General Hospital. A Supreme Court ruling in September 2025 found the hospital stay unlawful and not time served, and he was jailed on September 9, 2025. His possible return to politics is closely watched, and his son Paetongtorn's Pheu Thai party took a lesser role in the new conservative-led government after a poor 2026 election return.
