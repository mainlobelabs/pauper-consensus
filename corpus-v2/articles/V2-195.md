Proposed 2 World Trade Center breaks ground in Lower Manhattan
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 9, 2026 (via ABC News, Governor.ny.gov, Commercial Observer)

The proposed 2 World Trade Center has broken ground in Lower Manhattan, 25 years after the original building was destroyed during the September 11 attacks.

The 55-story, 1,226-foot tiered glass and steel tower at 200 Greenwich Street is the final commercial tower on the World Trade Center footprint, completing the 16-acre campus's commercial buildout. It will house the global headquarters of American Express, the sole owner and occupant, whose anchor deal was announced on 25 February.

Developer Silverstein Properties CEO Lisa Silverstein called the tower a "capstone for the World Trade Center." The architect is Foster + Partners, the contractor Turner, and completion is targeted for 2031, with steel top-out in 2029. The structure had sat dormant at street level since 2011, and the ceremonial kickoff followed construction that had already resumed.

New York mayor Zohran Mamdani, who spoke at the event, said the project would create 3,200 construction jobs and add about $11.4 billion to the city economy. Governor Kathy Hochul estimated $5.9 billion for the state economy, and PANYNJ chairman Kevin O'Toole called it "fulfilling a promise made 25 years ago to rebuild, to restore and to remember."
