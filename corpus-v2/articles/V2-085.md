Pope Leo XIV arrives in Algeria, the first papal visit to the country
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 13, 2026 (via Vatican News, AP)

Pope Leo XIV has arrived in Algiers, marking the first papal visit to Algeria. On Monday, April 13, 2026, the pope, the first Augustinian in the history of the papacy, landed in Algiers at about 10:30 a.m., and he is to visit Souk Ahras, the traditional birthplace of St. Augustine of Hippo, the namesake of the Augustinian Order.

On the first day, he visited the Maqam Echahid Martyrs' Monument, where he said "the future belongs to men and women of peace," then went to the Presidential Palace, delivered an address at the Djamaa El Djazair mosque, and visited the Great Mosque of Algiers, the third largest in the world, with its rector Al Qasimi for a moment of silent reflection.

He also visited the center of the Augustinian Missionary Sisters in Bab El Oued, where several of the sisters were killed during Algeria's civil war of 1994-96, and celebrated Mass at the Basilica of Our Lady of Africa. On the second day, he is to make a pilgrimage to the ruins of ancient Hippo and celebrate Mass at the Basilica of St. Augustine in Annaba, where Augustine lived, died, and wrote.

The trip is the first stop of an 11-day tour of four countries, including Cameroon, Angola, and Equatorial Guinea, that runs from April 13 to 23. The visit to North Africa underscores the pope's emphasis on interfaith dialogue.
