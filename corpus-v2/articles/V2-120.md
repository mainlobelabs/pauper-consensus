UN agencies warn of rapidly worsening hunger crisis and growing famine risk in Somalia
Wikipedia Current events portal, May 15, 2026 (via OCHA, FAO, WFP, UNICEF)

All UN agencies warned on Friday, May 15, 2026, that the Somali hunger and malnutrition crisis is rapidly worsening, with more than 6 million people, about 31 percent of the population, facing critical food insecurity and a growing risk of famine. The joint FAO, OCHA, UNICEF, and WFP assessment puts the country in emergency conditions for April through June 2026.

Nearly 1.9 million children suffer acute malnutrition, of whom about 493,000 face severe acute malnutrition and carry a 12-fold risk of death. The number facing Emergency conditions has tripled in under a year, and food prices have risen by up to 20 percent on fuel and maritime supply chains.

For the first time since the 2022 crisis, the UN's risk-of-famine analysis found that the agropastoral populations of Burhakaba district in Bay region face a risk of famine if the Gu season rains fail, food prices rise sharply, and aid is not scaled up, with about 40 percent of under-fives there already acutely malnourished. Drivers include severe drought, insecurity, limited humanitarian access, and the ripple effects of the Middle East conflict. The UN's Somalia representative called it a real and credible risk of famine in the district.
