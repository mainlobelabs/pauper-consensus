Sri Lankan court sentences former police chief Jayasundara to death over 2019 Easter bombings
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 31, 2026 (via AP, Al Jazeera, ABC, Straits Times)

A Sri Lankan court sentences former police chief Pujith Jayasundara to death for negligence in failing to prevent the 2019 Easter bombings, which killed 279 people; Sri Lanka has not carried out an execution since 1976.

A three-judge bench of the Colombo High Court, in a 2-1 majority, sentenced Jayasundara, 66, the former inspector general of police, to death for criminal neglect of duty in failing to act on imminent-attack intelligence. Former defence ministry secretary Hemasiri Fernando, 76, was also sentenced to death the same day.

The two were convicted on all but one of 855 charges, including criminal neglect and abetting murder. On Easter Sunday, 21 April 2019, IS-inspired bombs hit three churches and three luxury tourist hotels, killing 279 people including 45 foreigners.

An Indian agency had alerted Sri Lanka on 4 April 2019 to a possible suicide attack, and the then-SIS director Nilantha Jayawardena conveyed to Jayasundara on the evening of 20 April 2019 that suicide attacks targeting Catholic churches and tourist hotels were imminent. The two were acquitted in 2022 before the state appealed. The court ordered 125 million rupees in damages to survivors, and both can appeal to the Supreme Court.
