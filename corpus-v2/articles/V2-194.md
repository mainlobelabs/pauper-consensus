IMO chief: 6,000 seafarers still stranded in the Strait of Hormuz
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 8, 2026 (via IMO, Anadolu, UN News)

International Maritime Organization secretary-general Arsenio Dominguez has reported that hundreds of ships carrying around 6,000 seafarers remain stranded in the Persian Gulf since the beginning of the conflict, and he has called for maximum restraint and de-escalation amidst the renewal of the crisis.

"I condemn the attacks over the past two days against several ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz," Dominguez said. "These reckless attacks have again placed innocent seafarers in grave danger." He called on all states concerned to exercise maximum restraint, de-escalate the situation without delay, and facilitate the safe departure of the ships still trapped in the Gulf.

The evacuation plan that began in late June evacuated 136 vessels and 2,900 seafarers via two alternative routes before it was paused, because safety can no longer be assured. The existing traffic separation scheme is unusable because of sea mines.

The statement came as the IMO council met for its 137th session in London from 6 to 10 July. The IMO thanked Oman for its evacuation framework. Before the war that began on 28 February, the strait carried about 20 percent of the world's daily oil.
