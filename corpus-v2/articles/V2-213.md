US court sentences Sinaloa Cartel co-founder El Mayo to life in prison
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 20, 2026 (via DOJ, AP, BBC, Guardian)

A federal court in New York has sentenced Sinaloa Cartel co-founder El Mayo to life imprisonment and ordered 15 billion US dollars in forfeiture after he pled guilty to charges related to leading the cartel.

US District Judge Brian M. Cogan in Brooklyn federal court sentenced Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada Garcia, 76, to life in prison without parole, along with a 15 billion dollar forfeiture money judgment for drug-trafficking profits.

Zambada Garcia, who had pleaded guilty on 25 August 2025, was convicted on Count 1 of a fifth superseding indictment as a principal leader of a continuing criminal enterprise and on a RICO conspiracy count. He co-founded the Sinaloa Cartel with Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman, who was extradited in 2016 and sentenced to life in 2019. El Mayo served as the cartel's strategist and dealmaker, more involved in day-to-day operations than Guzman.

He was arrested in July 2024 in an elaborate sting operation, and US Attorney Joseph Nocella Jr. said the cartel had "nearly four decades poisoning American communities." Prosecutors said no assets had yet been identified or recovered. One of his sons remains a key leader of his faction, and the cartel has been at civil war since his arrest.
