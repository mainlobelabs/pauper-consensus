Andy Burnham wins the Makerfield by-election, setting up a leadership challenge to Keir Starmer
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 18, 2026 (via Guardian, Reuters, BBC)

Former Greater Manchester mayor Andy Burnham is elected to the House of Commons after winning a by-election in Makerfield, setting up a leadership challenge to prime minister Keir Starmer.

The by-election was held on Thursday, 18 June 2026, with the count early Friday. Burnham, 56, won Makerfield in Wigan with 24,927 votes, 54.8% of the 45,510, against Reform UK's Rob Kenyon, who got 34.5%. His majority of 9,231 was almost double his predecessor's. Restore Britain, a splinter group, came third with 3,111 votes.

The seat was vacated by Labour MP Josh Simons, who resigned after less than two years in office after Labour blocked his February bid for Gorton and Denton. Burnham, a former MP for Leigh from 2001 to 2017, pledged not to use the seat as a "stepping stone."

At the G7 summit in Versailles, Starmer said, "I will fight if there's a challenge... I'm not going to walk away." Labour had endured dismal May local results, and Starmer's approval had cratered, prompting no-confidence calls from MPs.

Wes Streeting, who resigned as health secretary in May, said he would join the contest. A challenge needs 81 Labour MPs, a fifth of the parliamentary party.
