Denmark's Frederiksen government bid fails; king asks Venstre's Poulsen to form a government
Wikipedia Current events portal, May 8, 2026 (via Guardian, Politico, The Local)

The bid of Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen to form a new government has failed, and on Friday, May 8, 2026, King Frederik X asked Troels Lund Poulsen, the leader of the Venstre party, to lead negotiations on forming a government that does not involve the participation of the Social Democrats or the Moderates, the Royal Household said.

Frederiksen's coalition talks broke off when key kingmaker Lars Løkke Rasmussen of the Moderates withdrew his support, saying he did not want a government reliant on the backing of the Red-Green Alliance. The near-deadlock March 24 election left the Social Democrats, who have governed since 2019, with a plurality but far short of a Folketing majority as 12 parties split the vote.

A King's Round at Amalienborg brought together the parties holding 87 seats, which recommended Poulsen, who also serves as deputy prime minister. Frederiksen said the Danes have composed the Folketing in such a way that a center-right government could well be formed. The formation process came amid Greenland and US tensions in the Nordic region, and with Poulsen's attempt set to follow, it is on track to become the longest government-formation process in Danish history.
