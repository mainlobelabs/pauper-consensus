Guterres appoints Rabab Fatima as next head of UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 15, 2026 (via UNAMA, Pajhwok, ReliefWeb)

UN secretary-general António Guterres has appointed Bangladeshi diplomat Rabab Fatima as the next head of the UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan, succeeding Roza Otunbayeva of Kyrgyzstan.

Guterres announced the appointment in a statement from New York on 15 July, naming Fatima as his new special representative for Afghanistan and head of UNAMA. He expressed gratitude to Otunbayeva for her service during a period of continued challenges in the country, and acknowledged the work of Gagnon and the mission staff. Gagnon, deputy special representative, is serving as officer-in-charge during the transition.

Fatima currently serves as UN under-secretary-general and high representative for least developed countries, landlocked developing countries, and small island developing states. She has a long career in diplomacy with the United Nations system. The decision marks the end of Otunbayeva's tenure at the head of the Kabul mission.

The appointment follows the Security Council's extension of UNAMA's mandate by one year on 15 June, under a China-sponsored resolution that was adopted with all 15 council members in favour. The mission, established in 2002, coordinates the UN's development and humanitarian work in Afghanistan from its headquarters in Kabul, and its mandate now runs through mid-2027.
