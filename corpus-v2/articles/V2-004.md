Azerbaijan sentences former Artsakh official to 20 years over war crimes
Wikipedia Current events portal, February 17, 2026 (via Reuters)

An Azerbaijani military court has sentenced former Republic of Artsakh state minister Ruben Vardanyan to 20 years in prison for terrorism, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, concluding the trial of one of the most prominent defendants from the former breakaway region.

The Baku military court handed down the sentence on Tuesday, February 17, 2026. Vardanyan, a billionaire businessman, was arrested in 2023 while leaving the region after Azerbaijan regained control.

The verdict was announced by the court sitting in Baku. It is one of a series of prosecutions of former officials of the Republic of Artsakh following the region's incorporation into Azerbaijan. Related defendants, including former leaders of the breakaway government, have received life sentences in separate proceedings.

The Republic of Artsakh, also known as Nagorno-Karabakh, had declared independence from Azerbaijan after the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991, and was internationally recognized as Azerbaijani territory. Azerbaijan's military regained control of the region in 2023.

The trials have drawn sustained attention from human rights organizations, which have monitored the cases of former Artsakh officials closely and raised questions about the legal basis of the charges. Vardanyan's case is among the highest-profile of the proceedings to date.
