Péter Magyar's Tisza wins two-thirds of Hungary's parliament, ending Orbán's 16-year rule
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 12, 2026 (via Reuters, BBC)

Hungarians have elected all 199 members of the National Assembly, with the Tisza Party of Péter Magyar winning two-thirds of the seats in the highest turnout election since the fall of communism. On Sunday, April 12, 2026, the final count gave Tisza 141 seats and 53.2 percent of the vote including the diaspora, the largest mandate in a free Hungarian election.

The opposition leader, 45, is a former Fidesz MEP who built his movement from within the governing party. The two-thirds supermajority, which requires 133 seats, allows Tisza to amend the constitution on its own. The ruling Fidesz, led by incumbent prime minister Viktor Orbán, won 52 seats and 38.6 percent, and the nationalist Mi Hazánk party took six seats.

Orbán conceded the defeat on election night, describing the result as "painful but clear." His defeat ends 16 years in power for Fidesz, which has governed Hungary continuously since 2010, and Magyar is expected to be tasked with forming the next government.

The final turnout was about 79 percent, the highest in a Hungarian parliamentary election since 1989. The 77.8 percent figure reported earlier was the count at 18:30, before the final results were in, and it had set a record at that time.
