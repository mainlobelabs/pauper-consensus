Sagrada Família reaches its designed height of 172.5 metres
Wikipedia Current events portal, February 20, 2026 (via AP, Vatican News)

The Sagrada Família basilica in Barcelona, Spain, has reached its designed height of 172.5 metres (566 feet) after a crane placed the upper arm of the cross on the Tower of Jesus Christ, the church's tallest element.

The milestone, announced on Friday, February 20, 2026, caps construction that began in 1882, more than 140 years ago, on the design of architect Antoni Gaudí, who died in 1926 with the work only partially complete.

The completion of the main building is expected later in 2026, according to reports surrounding the milestone. Interior work, including on the nave and the apse, is expected to continue through 2027 and 2028.

The crossing of the 172.5-metre mark brings the church's silhouette to its full intended profile, a goal that has driven successive generations of builders since the project began in the 19th century. The tower is planned to be crowned with a statue of the Virgin Mary.

The basilica, a UNESCO World Heritage site, has been under continuous construction since Gaudí took over the project in 1883, and its long build has in part been financed by ticket sales from the millions of visitors it receives each year.
