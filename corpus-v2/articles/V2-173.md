First US-Iran talks since the Islamabad Memorandum are held in Switzerland
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 21, 2026 (via The National, Dawn, AP)

The first talks between Iran and the US since the signing of the Islamabad Memorandum are held at the Bürgenstock Resort in Switzerland, mediated by Pakistan and Qatar.

The session, described as the "Lake Lucerne Summit," began on Sunday, 21 June 2026, as a quadrilateral with Pakistan and Qatar mediating and Switzerland facilitating. The US delegation was led by vice president JD Vance, with envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner handling technical matters. Iran was led by parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and foreign minister Abbas Araghchi.

The session paused for about 80 minutes for internal consultations and ran into the night and into Monday. Iranian negotiators at one point walked after Trump comments went public, demanding frozen-asset release and oil waivers before nuclear talks.

Pakistan and Qatar issued a joint statement on Monday describing a "positive and constructive atmosphere" and "encouraging progress" toward a mechanism for further technical talks. They agreed a high-level committee for political oversight, a roadmap to a final deal within 60 days, and working groups on nuclear, sanctions, and monitoring.

Vance said, "a little bit of threatening, a little bit of whining... the talks continued, and we made great progress."
