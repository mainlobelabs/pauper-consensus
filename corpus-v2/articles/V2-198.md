Canadian pilot killed in helicopter crash fighting the Gold Mountain Fire in Colorado
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 12, 2026 (via CBS Colorado, Guardian, Denver7, KUNC)

The Gunnison County Sheriff's Office has reported that Nicholas Dale, a 56-year-old Canadian pilot from Sooke, British Columbia, was killed after his Kaman K-1200 helicopter crashed into Silver Jack Reservoir while fighting the Gold Mountain Fire in Colorado.

The crash was reported around 5:17pm on Sunday, 12 July. The helicopter, a 2019 Kaman Aerospace K-Max owned by Georgia-based Helicopter Express LLC and flying under US Forest Service contract, went in inverted into the reservoir about 12 miles northeast of Ridgway. Dale was the sole occupant, and his body was recovered from the submerged helicopter by a Montrose County dive team.

The Gold Mountain Fire, north of Ouray, began on 27 June and has burned about 36,259 acres. The aircraft had been in Colorado since 6 July. The fire was 11 percent contained at the time the pilot's identity was released. Silver Jack Reservoir sits at an elevation of about 8,925 feet, and the K-Max is a single-engine turbine helicopter commonly used for firefighting bucket work. The NTSB plans to review the aircraft's cockpit video. A preliminary FAA report said the helicopter "crashed under unknown circumstances, becoming inverted."

Colorado governor Jared Polis ordered flags to half-staff in Dale's honour.
