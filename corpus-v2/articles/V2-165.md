Bank of Japan raises its interest rate to 1%, the highest level since 1995
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 16, 2026 (via BOJ, BBC, Nikkei Asia)

The Bank of Japan raises its interest rate to 1% from 0.75%, the highest level since 1995.

The two-day monetary policy meeting, which closed on Tuesday, 16 June 2026, voted 7-1 to lift the policy rate by 25 basis points, with Toichiro Asada, the first board member hand-picked by prime minister Sanae Takaichi, dissenting for a hold on the grounds that Middle East growth risks outweighed inflation. The deposit facility rate was set at 1.0% and the basic lending rate at 1.25%.

It is the first hike since December, when the bank raised the rate to 0.75%, and it had been widely expected. The energy-price surge from the US-Israel war with Iran, companies passing oil costs on at a "relatively fast pace," and a weak yen near 160 to the dollar drove the move, with underlying inflation approaching or exceeding the 2% target.

Deputy governor Shinichi Uchida said, "Price rises are broadening, and there is a risk that underlying inflation may deviate from our target."

The bank has been normalizing policy since March 2024, when it ended its 17-year negative-rate era, and the move aligns it with ECB tightening.
