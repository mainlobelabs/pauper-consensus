Iran names Arafi to interim leadership council after Khamenei's death
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 1, 2026 (via Al Jazeera, TRT)

Iran has appointed Ayatollah Alireza Arafi to the interim leadership council that has governed the country since the death of supreme leader Ali Khamenei.

The appointment, confirmed on Sunday, March 1, 2026, places Arafi, a member of the Guardian Council, into the interim arrangement that also includes president Masoud Pezeshkian and chief justice Gholam-Hossein Mohseni-Eje'i. The three-member body operates under Article 111 of the Iranian constitution, the provision that governs the vacancy of the office of supreme leader.

The council's authority was triggered on February 28, when Khamenei was killed in the opening attacks of the war between Iran and the United States and Israel. The triad now holds the leadership functions of the late supreme leader until the Assembly of Experts elects a successor.

Arafi has served on the Guardian Council, the clerical body that vets candidates for national elections. The interim arrangement is the first of its kind since the 1979 Islamic Revolution, when the office of supreme leader was created. The council is expected to oversee the country's affairs, including the conduct of the war, until a new supreme leader is chosen by the Assembly of Experts, a body of clerics that alone holds the power to make that selection.
