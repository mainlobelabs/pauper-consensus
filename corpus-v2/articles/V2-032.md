Australia grants asylum visas to five Iranian women footballers
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 10, 2026 (via BBC, CNN)

Australia has granted humanitarian visas to five members of Iran's women's national football team who sought asylum, including captain Zahra Ghanbari, Mona Hamoudi, Fatemeh Pasandideh, Atefeh Ramezanizadeh, and Zahra Sarbali.

Immigration minister Tony Burke signed off on the visas, which were completed in the early hours of Tuesday, March 10, 2026, local time. The five players had been in Australia taking part in the AFC Women's Asian Cup.

The players sought protection after refusing to sing Iran's national anthem before their March 1 group match against South Korea in Gold Coast, Queensland. The protest prompted a mob to surround the team bus at Gold Coast Airport, and the incident drew widespread attention to the players' situation.

The exiled heir to Iran's former throne, Reza Pahlavi, publicly pleaded for the players' protection. The visas allow the five to remain in Australia outside the tournament's player-protection framework. The Asian Cup is the premier women's tournament in the region and has drawn record television audiences across Asian markets. The Iranian team's stay in Australia was extended after the visa decisions were made, and support for the players' decision to seek asylum grew in the days after the anthem protest.
