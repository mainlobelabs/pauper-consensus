West Virginia declares state of emergency after flash floods
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 22, 2026 (via AP, CBS, NBC, Governor's office)

West Virginia governor Patrick Morrisey has declared a state of emergency and announced that 100 National Guard troops have been deployed after flash floods caused by torrential rains across the state.

Morrisey declared the state of emergency for all 55 counties on Tuesday night, after a state of preparedness on 21 July, and sent 100 National Guard members to the hardest-hit areas, mainly north-central Lewis and Upshur counties.

Torrential rains from a series of storm systems brought severe thunderstorms, damaging winds, and mudslides, causing flash floods that inundated homes and businesses, washed out bridges, and led to numerous water rescues. More than two inches of rain fell statewide on Saturday 18 July.

Nineteen guard and state police helicopters were on rescue or standby, and the state Department of Natural Resources took part in 85 rescues. Interstate 79 and US Route 48 were closed and later reopened. Two people were later confirmed dead after being swept away in an overturned vehicle. On Friday 25 July, Morrisey requested an expedited major disaster declaration from President Trump for Barbour, Doddridge, Harrison, Lewis, Pleasants, Ritchie, and Upshur counties. The guard deployment was later expanded to 500 members.
