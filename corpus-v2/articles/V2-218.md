Federal trial opens against Hadi Matar, who attempted to kill Salman Rushdie
Wikipedia Current events portal, July 22, 2026 (via AP, Reuters, Guardian, NBC)

The federal trial against Hadi Matar, the man who attempted to kill author Salman Rushdie in August 2022 in New York, opens; if convicted, Matar faces life in prison while already serving a 25-year state prison sentence.

The trial opened in Buffalo, New York, in the Western District. Matar, 28, who was born in the United States and holds Lebanese citizenship, is from Fairview, New Jersey. He pleads not guilty to federal charges including an act of transnational terrorism and material support for terrorists.

In his opening statement, Assistant US Attorney Timothy Lynch said Matar researched Rushdie's whereabouts for years and framed the killing as jihad to fulfil the 1989 fatwa over "The Satanic Verses." Lynch said Matar "wanted everyone to know he was doing this for Hezbollah," and noted a private note in which Matar wrote, "We need to kill him ASAP."

Matar stabbed Rushdie on stage at the Chautauqua Institution in August 2022, at a writers-in-political-exile event, leaving him blind in one eye. In the state case, he was convicted of attempted murder in February 2025 and sentenced to 25 years in May 2025. Rushdie is expected to testify at the federal trial.
