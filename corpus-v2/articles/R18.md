Wim Wenders withdraws The Wrong Move from distribution after Nastassja Kinski objects to a nude scene
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 3, 2026 (via AP, BBC, Variety)

German filmmaker Wim Wenders withdraws his 1975 film The Wrong Move from distribution after actress Nastassja Kinski objected to a nude scene filmed when she was 13 years old, calling for the film to be re-edited.

The Wim Wenders Foundation, which owns the rights, announced on Wednesday, 3 June 2026, that The Wrong Move would be withdrawn from "all current forms of distribution and exhibition," and that streaming services, television broadcasters, and distribution partners would be instructed to cease public access to the film.

In the scene, a mute teen acrobat lies topless in bed, and co-star Rüdiger Vogler, in his early 30s, strips to his underwear, lies on top of her, and slaps and caresses her face. Kinski, now 65 and the daughter of Klaus Kinski, had campaigned for about 15 years for a re-edit, saying, "That was my first film, he was my first director and he didn't protect me."

Wenders, 80, said in a statement, "As the only person responsible at the time for 'Wrong Move' who is still here, I recognize that Nastassja Kinski should have been better protected back then. For that, I apologize to you, Nastassja, unreservedly, no ifs and buts."

The film stays unavailable until a "mutually agreed solution" through broad dialogue. Kinski later worked with Wenders on Paris, Texas, which won the Palme d'Or in 1984, and Faraway So Close! in 1993.
