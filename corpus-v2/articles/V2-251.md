FDA approves daraxonrasib, first RAS inhibitor for metastatic pancreatic cancer
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 26, 2026 (via Reuters, CNN, Medscape, Dana-Farber)

The US Food and Drug Administration has approved the drug daraxonrasib as a treatment for metastatic pancreatic cancer, making it the first RAS inhibitor approved for the disease.

Daraxonrasib, sold as Rasonque and developed by Revolution Medicines, is an oral multi-selective inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family. The approval covers adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multi-agent systemic therapy.

The approval was based primarily on the pivotal randomized phase 3 RASolute 302 trial, which showed a survival benefit over chemotherapy. In the large study, the drug was shown to nearly double survival rates in patients with advanced disease, and the FDA granted accelerated approval.

RAS mutations drive about 90 percent of pancreatic cancers, but the proteins have long been considered undruggable. The approval marks the first targeted therapy for previously treated metastatic disease, and the Pancreatic Cancer Action Network called it the first FDA approval of a broadly effective RAS-targeted therapy.

Patients and advocacy groups welcomed the decision, and oncologists said the key question now is how the drug will be sequenced with existing chemotherapy regimens in routine practice.
