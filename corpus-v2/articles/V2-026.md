South Korea activates 100 trillion won fund to calm war-rattled markets
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 5, 2026 (via Bloomberg, Financial Times)

South Korean president Lee Jae Myung has ordered the activation of a 100 trillion won, about 68 billion US dollars, stabilization fund to counter financial-market volatility linked to the war in Iran.

The order, issued on Thursday, March 5, 2026, directs the rapid deployment of the fund into the country's financial markets as the conflict disrupts energy supplies and rattles regional trading. The stabilization fund is among the largest of its kind that any government has placed on standby.

The government is also weighing temporary fuel price caps to limit the cost-of-living impact at home. The measures arrive as the war's closure of the Strait of Hormuz has pushed energy prices higher across Asia.

South Korea imports the large majority of its oil and gas through the Strait of Hormuz, which makes the country especially exposed to disruption in the waterway. The finance ministry said it would coordinate with the central bank and the stock exchange on the fund's deployment. The stock exchange has prepared circuit-breaker measures for sharp declines, and the won has come under pressure against the dollar in recent trading sessions. The government said the fund would be used in concert with currency and bond-market tools if volatility persists.
