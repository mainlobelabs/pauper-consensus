El Salvador signs agreement with US to build its first nuclear plant
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 12, 2026 (via Energy for Growth)

El Salvador and the United States have signed Agreement 123 under the Atomic Energy Act of 1954, opening the way for the construction of a nuclear power plant in El Salvador, the country's first reactor.

The agreement, signed in March 2026, follows a memorandum of understanding between the two countries in February 2025. US officials have described the project's goal as "nuclear energy in seven years," a timeline that, measured from the signing, points to the early 2030s rather than the 2030 date cited in some reports.

A 123 Agreement is a US bilateral arrangement that authorizes cooperation on the peaceful use of nuclear energy with another country, and it is the legal prerequisite for the transfer of US nuclear technology and material.

El Salvador currently has no operating nuclear plants, and its electricity grid has long relied heavily on imported oil and gas, with geothermal power the largest renewable source. The new plant is intended to diversify the country's energy supply, and it would make El Salvador one of a small number of Central American countries to build a reactor, with the government citing energy independence as the central aim.
