Jerusalem's Old City holy sites reopen for the first time since the war began
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 9, 2026 (via AP, France24)

The Al-Aqsa Mosque, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, the Western Wall, and other holy sites in the Old City of Jerusalem have reopened for the first time since the war with Iran started on February 28. On Thursday, April 9, 2026, Israel lifted restrictions on all the holy sites, a move police announced the previous day, with hundreds of officers deployed.

The sites had been closed for 40 to 41 days, and Al-Aqsa was shuttered for most of Ramadan and the Eid al-Fitr holiday. The Al-Aqsa Mosque reopened at fajr, the dawn prayer, with an estimated 3,000 worshippers in attendance, according to the Wafa news agency.

The Western Wall and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre also reopened on the same day. The church, which holds sites believed to be central to the Christian narrative of the Passion of Christ, was to hold its candle-lighting Easter service on Saturday.

The reopening comes as the two-week ceasefire between Iran and the United States takes hold. The closure had been imposed on security grounds at the start of the war, and the lifting of the restrictions was coordinated among Israeli security forces and the custodians of the sites, marking the resumption of normal access for worshippers of all faiths in the Old City.
