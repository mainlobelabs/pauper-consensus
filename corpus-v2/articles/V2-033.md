IEA approves largest-ever oil reserve release
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 11, 2026 (via Axios, Financial Times)

The International Energy Agency has agreed to a discharge of 400 million barrels of oil from member strategic reserves, its largest-ever release, to counter soaring energy prices driven by the war.

The decision, reached by the agency's 32 members on Wednesday, March 11, 2026, is more than double the 182 million barrels released in 2022 after the invasion of Ukraine. It is the biggest coordinated reserve release in the agency's history.

The move comes as crude rises above 100 US dollars per barrel again, with the war disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway that carries a fifth of the world's oil. IEA member governments will draw down their strategic stocks to steady supplies and prices.

The IEA was created after the 1973 oil crisis, and its members are required to hold reserves and to act jointly when supply is disrupted. Past releases have included coordinated actions in 2005 and 2011. Analysts expect the 400 million barrels to be drawn down over several months rather than all at once, to avoid flooding the market and depressing prices too far. The agency has said the release will be reviewed as the situation in the Strait of Hormuz develops.
