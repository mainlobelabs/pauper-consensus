Cottonwood Fire burns over 59,000 acres in Utah, the most destructive in state history, governor says
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 24, 2026 (via SLT, Deseret News, KSL)

The Cottonwood Fire burns over 59,000 acres near the Eagle Point Resort in Beaver, Utah; governor Spencer Cox says it is the most destructive fire in the state's history.

The fire in the Tushar Mountains of Beaver County ignited on Monday, 22 June 2026, at about 3:36pm and was 0% contained as it grew. It destroyed the Eagle Point Resort, a private ski resort, losing the larger of its two day lodges, four of five lifts, and about half of its 600 acres.

Resort owner Shane Gadbaw said there was "significant property loss," and more than 300 homes were threatened. Mandatory evacuations were ordered for Eagle Point, Merchant Valley, HiLo Estates, and Arrowhead Summer Homes, with no deaths reported.

Governor Cox, who toured the fire on Wednesday, 24 June, said, "There's a very good chance this is already the most destructive fire in the state's history... It's certainly catastrophic." The state forester agreed, calling it likely the most destructive and costly fire the state has ever seen in terms of property damage.

The fire was one of 353 burning in Utah, fueled by a record drought and blustery winds. A FEMA major disaster declaration covered 75% of eligible costs, and more than 141,500 acres had burned in the state that year, a tenfold increase in a single week.
