ICC rules Tajikistan failed to arrest Putin during visit
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 19, 2026 (via Courthouse News, ICC)

The International Criminal Court has ruled that Tajikistan violated the Rome Statute by failing to arrest Russian president Vladimir Putin during his visit to the country in October 2025.

The finding, issued by the court's Pre-Trial Chamber II in The Hague on Thursday, March 19, 2026, found that Tajikistan, a state party to the treaty, failed to arrest and surrender Putin while he attended a Russia-Central Asia summit on October 9, 2025. The court referred the matter to the Assembly of States Parties, the treaty's governing body.

State parties to the Rome Statute are obliged to arrest anyone subject to the court's warrants when they enter their territory, and the findings are the court's mechanism for enforcing that obligation where a state declines to act. The Assembly will decide on any further measures.

The ruling follows similar findings in which the court determined that Mongolia had failed to arrest Putin during a 2024 visit, and that Hungary had failed to arrest Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The ICC has had an arrest warrant for Putin since 2023 over the alleged deportation of Ukrainian children, and the court has sought to use the findings to pressure state parties to comply with its orders.
