NASA names the Artemis III crew for a 2027 lunar lander docking test
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 9, 2026 (via NASA, SpaceNews, NPR)

NASA announces that astronauts Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, and Andre Douglas and ESA astronaut Luca Parmitano will crew the Artemis III mission in late 2027, whose goal is to evaluate rendezvous and docking operations with SpaceX's Starship HLS and Blue Origin's Blue Moon spacecraft.

The crewed test flight will launch on the Space Launch System and Orion from Kennedy Space Center for a roughly two-week campaign in low Earth orbit, modeled on the 1969 Apollo 9 mission. Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander will launch first on the New Glenn rocket and park in orbit, where Orion will dock for about two days while the crew crosses the hatch and tests life support. A test article of the SpaceX Starship lander will then dock with Orion for about a day before the crew re-enters and splashes down in the Pacific.

Bresnik, a retired US Marine Corps colonel, will command. Parmitano will be the first ESA astronaut on an Artemis mission. Rubio holds the US single-flight record at 371 days in space. Douglas will fly for the first time.

NASA administrator Jared Isaacman said the mission reduces risk before Artemis IV, the first crewed landing at the lunar South Pole planned for 2028. Both landers are required, and officials said the agency is not launching until the objectives bring the risk down.
