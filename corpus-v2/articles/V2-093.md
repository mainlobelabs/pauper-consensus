US justice department indicts Southern Poverty Law Center on fraud charges
Wikipedia Current events portal, April 21, 2026 (via AP, NBC)

The US justice department has indicted the Southern Poverty Law Center on fraud charges related to payments it made to infiltrate white supremacist hate groups, including the Ku Klux Klan, Aryan Nations, and the American Nazi Party. On Tuesday, April 21, 2026, a federal grand jury in Montgomery, Alabama, the Middle District Northern Division, returned an 11-count indictment.

The counts include six wire fraud, four false statements to federally insured banks, and one conspiracy to commit concealment money laundering. The SPLC, founded in 1971 and headquartered in Montgomery, is accused of secretly funneling more than USD 3 million of donor money between 2014 and 2023 to eight or nine unnamed "field sources" or informants in or affiliated with the KKK, the United Klans of America, Unite the Right, the National Alliance, the National Socialist Movement, the Sadistic Souls motorcycle club of Aryan Nations, the National Socialist Party of America, and the American Front.

The covert program has run since the 1980s, and the money moved via fictitious-entity accounts, including "Fox Photography" and "Rare Books Warehouse." The indictment includes two forfeiture actions. The investigation was conducted by the FBI and IRS Criminal Investigation, and one National Alliance informant received more than USD 1 million, while a Unite the Right Charlottesville chat-group member received more than USD 270,000 between 2015 and 2023.

Acting AG Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel held a press conference describing the organization as "manufacturing racism to justify its existence" and said the probe was "shut down" under the Biden administration before being revived in the Trump second term. SPLC interim CEO Bryan Fair said the group is "targeted."
