Defence Secretary John Healey resigns over UK military funding; Al Carns also quits
Wikipedia Current events portal, June 11, 2026 (via BBC, The Guardian, Independent)

John Healey resigns as UK Secretary of State for Defence in protest at a lack of government funding for the British Armed Forces, saying prime minister Keir Starmer is putting the country's security at risk; Parliamentary Under-Secretary Al Carns also resigns.

Healey, one of Starmer's most loyal cabinet allies, wrote on Thursday, 11 June 2026, that the prime minister was "unable" and the Treasury "unwilling" to commit the resources the nation needs to defend the country at a time of rising threats. He said the defence investment plan's financial settlement, given in full on Monday, "falls well short of what is required" and is backloaded, rising from 2.6% of GDP next year to 2.68% by 2030. Healey wanted 3% by 2030.

He said he was "forced to make decisions that would reduce the readiness of our forces... and could make our country less safe." Reports had said the government offered only £13.5 billion over four years against the Ministry of Defence's request for £28 billion, with about 1% capital-budget cuts across departments.

Starmer replied in a letter that the government has delivered the highest sustained increase in defence spending since the Cold War in a sustainable way and that "irresponsible borrowing only puts that at risk."

Dan Jarvis, a former Army officer serving as security minister, was named Healey's successor on Thursday evening. Al Carns resigned hours later, saying the plan was "neither transformative enough nor sufficiently funded."
