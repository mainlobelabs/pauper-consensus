Trial opens in London for two British-Chinese nationals in Hong Kong spying case
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 4, 2026 (via BBC, Metropolitan Police)

A trial has opened at the Central Criminal Court in London over two British-Chinese nationals charged with spying on diasporic Hong Kongers on behalf of the China-appointed Hong Kong government.

The defendants, Bill Yuen and Peter Wai, were originally charged in May 2024 under the UK National Security Act 2023 for running a network that monitored pro-democracy Hong Kongers living abroad, with a third person, Trickett, originally charged alongside them. The trial began on March 2, 2026.

The prosecution must show that the defendants assisted a foreign intelligence service in carrying out surveillance on people in the United Kingdom. The case is one of the first major prosecutions brought under the 2023 act, which created a new offense for foreign interference in UK affairs.

The defendants worked for the Hong Kong trade office in London, which the UK government has described as a front for Chinese intelligence activity. The office had run a network that kept a register of pro-democracy Hong Kongers in the diaspora and reported on their activities. The court is scheduled to hear testimony over several weeks about the monitoring of diaspora Hong Kongers, and the case has been watched closely by London's Chinese diaspora community.
