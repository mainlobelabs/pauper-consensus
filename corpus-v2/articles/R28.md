NFL announces elimination of the Pro Bowl, ending a tradition dating to 1951
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 26, 2026 (via AP, The Guardian, USA Today)

In American football, the National Football League has announced the elimination of the Pro Bowl, ending the exhibition of the league's top players that was held in some form nearly every year since 1951.

The league announced the scrapping of the AFC-NFC Pro Bowl game but said players will still be honored as Pro Bowlers. The honors will take place during Super Bowl week in Los Angeles, and 88 Pro Bowlers will still be selected.

Starting with the 2026 season, Pro Bowlers will be chosen under a new no-replacement rule, meaning that if a selected player is injured, his spot will not be filled. The first class will be named in late December.

The 2026 Pro Bowl Games, held on 3 February 2026 during Super Bowl LX week, was the last game. That edition was the fourth season of the non-contact flag football format and the first to be integrated into the Super Bowl festivities. The event had previously evolved from a full-contact tackle game into the Pro Bowl Games in 2023, and then into flag football.

USA Today noted that the Pro Bowl was already dead, and the league has cited shifting fan interest and low broadcast ratings for the decision. The announcement followed years of format changes and declining attendance at the exhibition.
