Belarusian activist Maria Kolesnikova receives Charlemagne Prize in Aachen
Wikipedia Current events portal, March 14, 2026 (via AP, DW)

Belarusian opposition activist Maria Kolesnikova has received the International Charlemagne Prize in the German city of Aachen, awarded for her role in the 2020 Belarusian protests against president Alexander Lukashenko.

Kolesnikova was handed the prize on Saturday, March 14, 2026. She was named a recipient in 2022, jointly with fellow opposition figures Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya and Viktar Tsepkalo, but was in prison at the time and could not attend.

Kolesnikova was released from prison in December 2025, after serving part of an 11-year sentence handed down following her arrest during the 2020 protests, which were triggered by disputed election results.

The Charlemagne Prize is awarded each year by the city of Aachen to individuals who have made an outstanding contribution to European unity, and it is one of the most prestigious civic honors in Germany, with past recipients drawn from politics, science and the arts across Europe. Kolesnikova was one of the most prominent figures in the 2020 movement, and her release marked a shift in the treatment of political prisoners in Belarus. The prize money will be used for a fund supporting opposition figures and their families who remain in detention or exile. The award ceremony in Aachen was attended by diplomats from several European capitals.
