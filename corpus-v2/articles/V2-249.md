Romania shuts down second reactor at Cernavoda as Danube drops to record low
Wikipedia Current events portal, August 13, 2026 (via AP, BBC, Euronews, The Guardian)

Romania has begun shutting down the second reactor at the Cernavoda Nuclear Power Plant, the country's only nuclear power plant, after drought drove the Danube below the level needed for cooling, leaving both reactors offline.

A severe drought gripping Europe has pushed the Danube to a record low. The river is the plant's cooling water source, and reactor 1 had already been shut down earlier this month. The controlled shutdown of the second reactor, operated by Nuclearelectrica, began on Wednesday.

Cernavoda normally produces around one-fifth of Romania's electricity. The government said alternative generation and imports will cover electricity demand, and the country has been forced to import more power as the energy crisis deepens.

The shutdown is part of the broader 2026 European heat wave and drought, which has deepened fears of an energy crisis across the continent. Hungary, also facing low water levels, has dammed sections of the Danube.

The Romanian government said the reactor will restart once water levels recover, and it urged the public to conserve electricity. The episode has raised questions about the resilience of European nuclear plants to prolonged dry spells.
